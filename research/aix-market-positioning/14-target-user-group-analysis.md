The money-relationship distinction matters. The Kansas City Fed function model assigns stablecoin activity across trading assets (48.8%), transfers (29.3%), idle balances (21.2%), and payments (0.7%) [L]. Stablecoin activity therefore does not by itself prove an everyday-money relationship.

## 3. Competitive archetypes / who owns what

Mechanisms and product types are separated here from the job map.

### Card-first crypto spend

**RedotPay / Bitget / ether.fi / Oobit** [T][G]

RedotPay is reported at more than 8.5m users across 100 countries and approximately $14B in annual payment volume [G]. In the PH stablecoin-starting card segment, RedotPay, Bitget, ether.fi Direct Pay, and Oobit already exist. Card issuance, acceptance, top-up, and cashback are highly similar [T].

Another stablecoin Visa/Mastercard card is an entry ticket, not differentiation. This archetype is crowded and commoditized.

### Broad stablecoin money account

**KAST** [H]

KAST is reported at more than 1m users and close to $5B in annualized volume. Its broad earn globally → hold digitally → spend locally / global stablecoin money-account position is already occupied [H].

### Fiat-first global money

**Wise / Revolut / banks / e-wallets** [D]

Wise is reported at 18.9m active users, $243.5B in cross-border volume, $39B in holdings, $43.6B in card spend, and 75% of Q4 payments completing in less than 20 seconds [D]. The archetype is a strong fiat-first cross-border/global-money alternative.

### Payout / distribution

**Deel / Stripe / Remote** [P][N][O][C]

Deel has a stablecoin wallet across 80+ countries for get paid → hold a dollar-backed balance → spend [P]. Stripe and Remote have stablecoin payout coverage across 60+ countries [O]. Stripe research covers more than 2,300 workers across 20 countries; 57% of the full survey reported willingness to be paid in stablecoins, while 18% is the emerging-market subgroup’s current stablecoin payout/use signal [N]. BVNK/Deel provides a provider-linked floor of 10,000+ opt-ins across 100+ markets [C].

### Local purchasing rails

**Coins.ph / GCash / Maya / BSP QRPh** [S]

Coins.ph has direct stablecoin-to-QRPH purchasing. GCash and Maya represent a two-step pattern: crypto → PHP wallet → QR/bills. BSP QRPh is a mainstream local retail rail [S]. Direct and two-step local purchasing must be treated as different mechanisms.

PH local rails are therefore not an empty field. Rail presence alone does not prove a differentiated money relationship.

### Transport-first remittance

**Stablecoin as a remittance bridge** [K]

The remittance context includes 367 corridors, a 6.36% average cost, and approximately $685B for LMICs. In a bounded sender-side study of n=866, 26% had adopted stablecoin for remittance [K]. This is sender-side bounded evidence: sender ≠ recipient, and it does not prove recipient retained or reused a balance. Stablecoin can be only a bridge or invisible rail; the recipient may cash out immediately.

Argentina and Türkiye are validation contexts only, not route or launch-market decisions [A][Q][R].

## 5. White-space hypotheses

### H1 — Digital-dollar value continuity → country-specific local everyday purchasing power depth

- Legacy F is mapped to H1.
- The core is value continuity until use plus country-specific local-money depth; it is not the number of rails.
- H1 differentiation is a hypothesis only.
- There is currently no evidence proving that KAST or local wallets leave this residual gap.
- **Status: Plausible / NOT_YET_PASS.**

H1 leads discovery because [I] supports a structural value-protection rationale in EMDEs, [O] links contractor payout demand directionally to holding/spending USD to avoid local-currency swings and FX fees, and [B] provides bounded signals of everyday activity (27%), stablecoin holding (60% among emerging crypto-engaged respondents), and conversion or spending within days (28%) in a crypto-engaged cohort. Together these are positive but limited signals consistent with value protection, payout → hold/spend, and an everyday-use / hold-until-use hypothesis. They support prioritizing H1 for validation only; they do not prove a KAST/local-wallet residual gap, a moat, or AIX Right-to-win.

H1 must be tested in a specific geography and locally dominant money context. It cannot be promoted from a general crypto-adoption signal.

### H2 — Destination-side retained digital-dollar relationship after cross-border income/remittance

- The hypothesis is that, after receipt, value is retained, managed, and locally reused rather than immediately cashed out.
- The evidence is weaker than for H1.
- The relationship, frequency, and differentiated outcome remain Unknown.

### H3 — Recurring global income as anchor for downstream money relationship

- Upstream payout is occupied by Deel, Stripe, and related distribution alternatives [P][N][O][C].
- Any AIX opportunity would be only in the receipt-after lifecycle.
- Independent differentiation is Unknown.

## 7. Differentiation challenge: H1 versus adjacent positions

### H1 versus KAST

KAST already occupies broad earn globally → hold digitally → spend locally [H]. H1 can only be narrower: specific-country local-money depth plus value continuity until use. There is no evidence proving that a residual gap remains after KAST or local-wallet alternatives. If matched competitor/local-market evidence shows that KAST or local wallets are sufficient, abandon H1.

### H1 versus RedotPay

The card-first field is crowded and commoditized, so H1 should not lead with a card route [T][G]. That conclusion does not prove that H1 has a moat.

### H1 versus Wise

H1 is not fiat-first. Its starting money is digital-dollar/stablecoin value. Whether users consider it worthwhile to preserve that value until use, rather than convert first, is a hypothesis. Wise remains a strong fiat-first alternative [D].

### H1 versus Deel / Stripe / Remote

H1 does not need to compete for upstream distribution. Any possible opportunity would be in the post-receipt lifecycle: what happens after a digital-dollar or stablecoin value is received. Whether that lifecycle is differentiated is Unknown [P][N][O][C].

### H1 versus remittance rails

H1 is not transport-first. A deeper relationship exists only if destination-side retained and repeated local use is real; a stablecoin bridge followed by immediate cash-out is not enough [K].

## 8. Candidate-derived user definition

This is a candidate-derived definition, not a validated target segment.

- **Money Source:** meaningful recurring cross-border/digital income, stablecoin receipts, or existing digital-dollar holdings. The concrete source differs by user.
- **Source Currency:** stablecoin/digital dollar, or upstream fiat already converted into stablecoin. Do not assume the wage currency.
- **Preferred Money Form:** the user wants at least part of the value to remain digital-dollar/stablecoin until local use, rather than fully cashing out every time.
- **Core Job:** 把全球/数字美元资金低摩擦地变成我所在国家每天真正能用的购买力，同时不用先把全部资金变成本币。
- **Why alternatives fail:** Hypothesis / Unknown. The failure mode must be demonstrated against KAST, RedotPay, Wise, Deel/Stripe/Remote, and relevant local wallets or rails.
