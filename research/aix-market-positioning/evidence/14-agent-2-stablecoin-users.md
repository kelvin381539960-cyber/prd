## Reading rules

- The unit is a repeated money behavior and its underlying Job, not a nationality, occupation label, wallet address, product, or vendor. The same person can appear in more than one card over time; the cards are not additive market counts.
- `Source Currency` means the currency or asset before the stablecoin step. `Preferred Money Form` means the form the user wants to keep or use after receiving, converting, or transferring. One must not be inferred from the other.
- `Fact` is directly stated in a source; `User Evidence` is reported survey or cohort behavior; `Market Evidence` is modeled, provider-reported, or network-level evidence; `Inference` is a bounded synthesis; `Hypothesis` requires direct validation; `Unknown` means the supplied evidence cannot establish the claim.
- Why AIX is deliberately conservative. Without direct AIX-specific differentiation evidence, the answer is `Unknown / opportunity hypothesis`; it does not use UX quality, one-stop positioning, or convenience as a reason.
- This file stays at Strategy + Capability Level. It does not prioritize Remittance, QR, Send, Card, Wallet, or any other capability, and it does not define features, pages, APIs, vendors, partners, owners, commercial terms, or implementation.

## Card S1 — Recurring stablecoin-paid global digital earners / contractors / Web3 workers

**Card status:** Candidate / unranked. No Primary selection.

### 1. Segment Definition

**Fact / analyst definition:** An individual who repeatedly receives compensation for work in a stablecoin. The work may be digital freelancing, contracting, creator or marketplace work, or a Web3 service. The defining behavior is recurring stablecoin-paid compensation; a one-off transfer, airdrop, generic crypto income, or fiat-paid worker does not qualify. The specific Web3-worker share is **Unknown**. [E01–E03]

### 2. Core Jobs

- Receive employer, client, or platform compensation across borders.
- Access a portable dollar-like receiving form when the payer and worker are in different markets.
- Decide whether each pay event should be held, converted, transferred, or spent.
- Connect recurring work income to the worker’s wider money obligations without losing control of timing or form.

These are Job-level statements. The evidence does not establish which downstream action dominates.

### 3. Money Source

**Fact:** Employer, client, or platform compensation for work. BVNK reports that contractors opted into stablecoin pay through a Deel-related payroll arrangement. The broader survey category “stablecoin income” is not a clean salary or work-compensation denominator and must not be relabeled as such. [E01–E02]

### 4. Source Currency

**Fact / Unknown:** USD, EUR, and GBP are explicitly described as employer funding currencies in the BVNK/Deel example. The worker-level source-currency mix, local-currency origin, and currency mix for freelance, creator, marketplace, and Web3 subgroups are **Unknown**. Stablecoin is the receiving form in the observed arrangement, not the source currency. [E02]

### 5. Preferred Money Form

**Inference / Unknown:** Opting into stablecoin pay supports a preference for stablecoin at the receiving step for the reported cohort. The preferred form after receipt is **Unknown**: the worker may retain a stablecoin balance, convert to local fiat, use bank/e-wallet money, or use another money form. USD/EUR/GBP employer funding must not be treated as evidence of the worker’s preferred post-receipt form. [E02–E03]

### 6. Current Financial Behavior

**User Evidence / Market Evidence:** The observed behavior is recurring compensation receipt through a stablecoin payroll or payout path. After receipt, the worker may hold, convert, transfer, or spend; the supplied sources do not provide a worker-level split of those outcomes. The evidence therefore supports a recurring income event, not a durable stablecoin financial relationship. [E01–E02]

### 7. Current Crypto Behavior

**Fact / Unknown:** The worker receives stablecoin as compensation. There is no direct evidence that the worker trades volatile assets, uses DeFi, holds a second crypto asset, or remains crypto-native after receiving payment. USDT/USDC mix, custody pattern, and whether the worker buys stablecoin independently are **Unknown**. The 39% survey income signal is broader than this card. [E01–E02]

### 8. Stablecoin Motivation

**Market Evidence / Inference / Unknown:** Plausible motivations are cross-border portability, access, settlement speed, and reducing perceived payment friction. The survey reports lower fees, security, and global access as leading payment motivations among its crypto-engaged sample, but it does not establish the individual reason for stablecoin payroll adoption. Direct worker-level motivation is **Unknown**. [E01]

### 9. Pain Severity

**Inference / Unknown:** Cross-border and high-inflation compensation contexts can create **medium–high category-level pain** around access, timing, conversion, or value form. Severity for this specific cohort is **Unknown** because the supplied sources do not measure failed payments, time lost, fee burden, or willingness to switch at the worker level. The existence of strong fiat alternatives also means receiving pain may not translate into a need to retain stablecoin.

### 10. Frequency

**Inference:** Recurring by definition, normally aligned with payroll or invoice cycles. Actual weekly/monthly cadence, pay amount, number of payers, and stablecoin share of total income are **Unknown**. The segment definition must not be weakened to include a single payout.

### 11. Economic / Relationship Value

**User Evidence / Hypothesis:** Recurring compensation can have high economic relevance to the worker and creates a plausible repeat money relationship. The survey reports that stablecoin income represented approximately 35% of annual earnings for a reported cohort, but the denominator is crypto-engaged, self-reported, and broader than salary. Relationship value is therefore **potentially medium–high**, while balance retention and downstream money value are **Unknown**. [E01–E02]

### 12. Current Alternatives

**Market Evidence / Inference:** Fiat payroll, bank transfer, payroll/payment platforms, multi-currency accounts, local e-wallets, ordinary cards, remittance services, and cash-out through an exchange are plausible alternatives. Wise is an example of a scaled cross-border alternative; its reported scale does not prove stablecoin substitution or worker preference. Relative alternative share for this card is **Unknown**. [E12]

### 13. Why AIX

**Unknown / opportunity hypothesis:** No supplied evidence directly shows that AIX reaches recurring stablecoin-paid workers or produces a materially better outcome for their compensation Job than existing alternatives. Strategically, the recurring income event could be a candidate anchor for a durable money relationship, but that is a hypothesis about the Job—not evidence of AIX differentiation, current fit, or product priority.

### 14. Why NOT AIX

There is no direct AIX-specific evidence for reach, eligibility, worker trust, switching, or differentiated economic outcome. A worker may choose stablecoin only at receipt and immediately cash out or transfer it; in that case the relationship is a payout event rather than a retained money balance. Strong fiat and cross-border alternatives, plus Unknown geography and regulatory eligibility, are sufficient reasons not to treat AIX fit as established.

### 16. Reachability

**Market Evidence / Unknown:** Existing stablecoin-paid workers may have lower awareness friction because they already encounter the form. However, the provider’s 100+ country distribution is not evidence of AIX geographic eligibility, acquisition access, trust, or switching. AIX-specific reachability is **Unknown**; no country or corridor is selected as a target market here. [E02–E03]

### 17. Retention / Money Relationship

**Hypothesis / Unknown:** Repeated pay events create a plausible recurring relationship, but direct linked fund retention is unproven. A broad survey signal that 28% of holders convert or spend within days is consistent with immediate disposition, but is not payroll-specific and cannot be used as a worker retention rate. The key unresolved distinction is recurring receipt versus intentional balance held between pay events. [E01]

### 18. Evidence Strength

**Overall: Medium for the existence of a recurring stablecoin-paid cohort; Low–Medium for recurrence beyond the reported cohort; Low for post-receipt preference, market size, AIX reachability, and durable retention.** The strongest evidence is provider-reported opt-in compensation and a crypto-engaged survey; neither is an independent global payroll census or longitudinal balance study. [E01–E03]

### Key Disconfirming Evidence

- “Stablecoin income” in the survey is not a clean salary, contractor, or Web3-worker denominator. [E01]
- The 10,000+ contractor figure is provider-reported opt-in adoption, not an independent market total and not proof of retained balances. [E02]
- Deel evidence about preference for USD or dollar-like compensation in high-inflation markets does not equal stablecoin preference. [E03]
- Immediate conversion, transfer, or spending can make stablecoin a payout rail without making it the worker’s preferred money balance. [E01–E02]
- Scaled fiat and cross-border alternatives can satisfy the same Job, so stablecoin receipt alone does not establish an AIX opportunity. [E12]

### Validation Gate

**Status: NOT PASSED / candidate remains unranked.** Strategic validation requires:

1. Direct worker-level evidence that compensation is recurring, stablecoin-paid, and tied to employment, contracting, creator, marketplace, or Web3 work—not generic crypto income.
2. A source-currency and post-receipt study that separately measures hold, cash-out, transfer, and spend behavior, including whether a balance remains between pay events.
3. Evidence of worker pain, alternative choice, willingness to switch, and AIX-specific reachability in clearly bounded corridors or markets.
4. A falsification check: if most workers cash out immediately, stablecoin is not preferred after receipt, or existing fiat alternatives dominate without material residual pain, keep AIX fit **Unknown**.

## Card S2 — High-inflation / FX-friction digital-dollar or stablecoin savers

**Card status:** Candidate / unranked. No Primary selection.

### 1. Segment Definition

**User Evidence / Market Evidence / analyst definition:** An individual who converts or holds a digital dollar—especially USDT or USDC in the evidenced Argentina context—primarily to protect the nominal value of earnings or savings against inflation, devaluation, FX friction, or restricted access to dollar-like money. The primary Job is value protection and access, not trading, yield, routine spending, or a temporary transfer bridge. [E05–E09]

### 2. Core Jobs

- Preserve nominal value when local currency is deteriorating or access to foreign currency is constrained.
- Obtain a USD-like savings form that is available when the user needs it.
- Decide when to convert local earnings or savings and when to return funds to a spendable local form.
- Keep optionality for future household obligations without making the balance a trading position.

### 3. Money Source

**User Evidence / Inference:** Local-currency earnings and savings are the evidenced source in the Argentina use case. The user’s underlying funds are wages, savings, or both; other source-of-funds paths and the household share of income are **Unknown**. Stablecoin is the chosen holding form in the observed use case, not the original money source. [E06]

### 4. Source Currency

**User Evidence / Inference / Unknown:** ARS/local fiat is the explicit source-currency context for Argentina. In Nigeria and other high-inflation or FX-friction markets, local currency and FX pressure are part of the context, but the source-currency mix is **Unknown**. No source supports projecting ARS or any single local currency across all markets. [E06–E09]

### 5. Preferred Money Form

**User Evidence:** USDT/USDC are named as the digital-dollar form used in the Argentina paycheck and savings-protection context. This supports a use-case-specific preference for a USD-denominated stablecoin form, not a universal token preference. Physical USD, bank-held USD, local cash, other assets, and the USDT-versus-USDC choice outside that context are **Unknown**. [E06]

### 6. Current Financial Behavior

**Market Evidence / User Evidence:** The observed behavior is conversion or holding of stablecoin for paycheck and savings protection, with a possible later conversion for obligations. The cadence, balance size, holding duration, share of household savings, and actual cash-out behavior are **Unknown**. The Kansas City Fed’s 21.2% “idle” estimate cannot be relabeled as savers because it can include rarely used, forgotten, or small balances. [E04, E06–E09]

### 7. Current Crypto Behavior

**User Evidence / Market Evidence:** This card requires actual stablecoin acquisition or holding for value protection, but does not imply trading, DeFi, yield, or broad crypto ownership. Argentina evidence names USDT/USDC; the token mix elsewhere is **Unknown**. Nigeria’s cited centralized-exchange fiat-purchase mix—approximately 89% BTC and 7% USDT—shows why high inflation or crypto activity cannot be used as a shortcut for stablecoin preference. [E05–E09]

### 8. Stablecoin Motivation

**User Evidence / Market Evidence:** The direct motivation is to protect paychecks or savings from local-currency deterioration and obtain USD-like access. Secondary motivations may include access and liquidity, but the supplied evidence does not measure which factor dominates for each saver. This is a value-protection motivation, distinct from trading liquidity or yield. [E06–E09]

### 9. Pain Severity

**Inference / Unknown:** Pain is **potentially high at category level** in markets with persistent inflation, devaluation, capital/FX friction, or restricted access to dollar-like savings. Severity for the specific user, including loss avoided, alternative access, and willingness to pay or switch, is **Unknown**. The evidence should not generalize high pain from Argentina or selected African contexts to every market.

### 10. Frequency

**Inference / Unknown:** Conversion may recur when income arrives or FX risk changes; holding is intended to persist across events. Actual conversion cadence, holding duration, balance size, and withdrawal frequency are **Unknown**. A single stablecoin purchase is insufficient to classify a recurring saver without evidence of an ongoing value-protection Job.

### 11. Economic / Relationship Value

**Hypothesis:** The economic value can be high where the user is protecting meaningful income or savings from nominal loss. The intentional-savings Job creates a plausible balance relationship with stronger retention potential than a just-in-time transfer, but no longitudinal source proves duration, balance persistence, or that stablecoin is retained instead of physical/bank USD or another asset. Relative attractiveness is not decided in this card.

### 12. Current Alternatives

**Inference / Unknown:** Physical USD, bank-held foreign currency, formal FX access, local savings instruments, other dollarized assets, cash, and crypto assets such as BTC are plausible alternatives. Their relative usage, availability, trust, and cost are not measured consistently across the cited markets. The Nigeria purchase mix is a counter-signal against assuming stablecoin is the default alternative. [E07]

### 13. Why AIX

**Unknown / opportunity hypothesis:** No supplied evidence directly shows that AIX reaches high-inflation or FX-friction savers or delivers a differentiated value-protection outcome. If direct user evidence confirms an intentional stablecoin savings relationship, the segment could be relevant to a retained-money hypothesis; that is not evidence of current AIX fit, capability, or priority.

### 14. Why NOT AIX

There is no AIX-specific evidence for market reach, eligibility, trust, retention, or differentiation. Users may prefer physical or bank-held USD, BTC, local instruments, or an existing crypto venue; they may also cash out when an obligation arises. “High inflation” does not prove stablecoin demand, and stablecoin holding can be trading, transfer, forgotten balance, or another role. These unresolved alternatives and role ambiguities prevent an AIX conclusion.

### 15. Market Size

| Layer | Assessment | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Category** | Inflation-, devaluation-, and FX-friction-driven savings / USD-access demand is a broad category, but no comparable total of affected savers is supplied. | Macro pressure or country examples are not converted into a user TAM. |
| **Relevant** | Chainalysis documents Argentina paycheck/savings protection and reports stablecoins above half of some local-currency exchange purchases in LATAM markets; related African evidence identifies FX/inflation-protection use. | Exchange-purchase shares and market narratives are not unique saver counts, savings balances, or household penetration. [E06–E09] |
| **Reachable** | AIX-addressable savers who are stablecoin-aware, eligible, able to acquire/hold the form, and willing to switch: **Unknown**. | Observed use-case geography is not AIX distribution or reachable market size. |
| **Unknown** | Unique savers; country-by-country denominator; stablecoin share of savings; balance and duration; USDT/USDC split; source-currency mix; alternative choice; household-spend and cash-out behavior; AIX-addressable share. | No supplied source closes these gaps. |

### 16. Reachability

**Market Evidence / Unknown:** Qualitative reach is more plausible where stablecoin is already used for FX or inflation protection, including the cited Argentina and Sub-Saharan Africa contexts. There is no evidence that AIX can access those users, meet their eligibility needs, or earn trust at scale. AIX-specific reachability is **Unknown**; no geography is selected here. [E06–E09]

### 17. Retention / Money Relationship

**Hypothesis / Unknown:** This is a plausible intentional-balance relationship because the Job is to preserve value rather than route funds immediately. It is not proven: no cited source measures holding duration, cross-period balance persistence, or retention with the same provider. The 21.2% modeled idle share is not direct saver retention, and it may include forgotten or small balances. [E04]

### 18. Evidence Strength

**Overall: Medium–Strong for the existence and motive in named markets; Low for cross-market scale, balance duration, household penetration, token split, AIX reachability, and retention.** The strongest evidence is context-specific user/market analysis from Argentina and regional analytics; it is not a representative multi-country saver panel. [E05–E09]

### Key Disconfirming Evidence

- Nigeria’s cited 2025 CEX fiat-purchase mix is approximately 89% BTC versus 7% USDT, so inflation pressure does not imply stablecoin preference. [E07]
- The Kansas City Fed’s 21.2% idle category includes balances that may be small, forgotten, or rarely used; it is not a saver population or retention measure. [E04]
- Stablecoin activity can be trading, transfer, or tactical liquidity rather than savings; the 48.8% trading-assets estimate is counter-evidence to treating every holder as a saver. [E04–E05]
- Physical USD, bank-held USD, BTC, local instruments, or existing crypto venues may satisfy the same value-protection Job.
- If users convert immediately or cannot retain a balance, the value relationship may be episodic rather than durable.

### Validation Gate

**Status: NOT PASSED / candidate remains unranked.** Strategic validation requires:

1. Direct user evidence in more than one clearly bounded high-inflation or FX-friction context that the primary Job is value protection, not trading, yield, transfer, or routine spending.
2. Separate measurement of source currency, preferred money form, token choice, conversion cadence, balance duration, and alternative store-of-value behavior.
3. Longitudinal evidence of intentional retained balances or repeated save/withdraw cycles, not an idle-wallet proxy.
4. AIX-specific evidence of reachability and a differentiated outcome. If most users prefer BTC, cash/bank USD, another asset, or immediate conversion, keep AIX fit and segment status **Unknown**.

## Card S3 — Recurring stablecoin remittance / family-money sender

**Card status:** Candidate / unranked. Sender only; no recipient segment is included. No Primary selection.

### 1. Segment Definition

**Fact / analyst definition:** An individual who repeatedly initiates personal or family support across a national border where stablecoin is part of the transfer path. This card covers the **sender**, not the recipient. It excludes a recipient-only user, a self-transfer user, and a commercial settlement user when those are the primary behavior. Stablecoin may be acquired and forwarded quickly; holding stablecoin is not required by the definition.

### 2. Core Jobs

- Send family or personal support reliably across borders.
- Reduce time, cost, FX, access, or intermediary friction in a recurring obligation.
- Control the timing and form of the sender-side transfer.
- Increase the probability that the recipient can reach a usable local money form, without treating the recipient as part of this card.

The last Job describes the sender’s outcome concern; it does not create a recipient analysis or recipient evidence claim.

### 3. Money Source

**Inference / Unknown:** Sender wages, savings, or other personal income are plausible sources of family support. The supplied evidence does not provide a person-level split by wages, savings, business income, or other source. The recipient’s household source is out of scope for this sender card.

### 4. Source Currency

**Unknown:** The sender may begin with local fiat, USD, another fiat currency, or another asset before the stablecoin step. Stablecoin is treated as a transfer instrument, not assumed to be the source currency. No cited source supplies a comparable sender-level source-currency mix across corridors.

### 5. Preferred Money Form

**Inference / Unknown:** The sender may prefer stablecoin as a transfer bridge because of access, timing, cost, or FX considerations. The sender’s preferred retained form after sending is **Unknown**. The final payout form is a recipient-side question and is not used to classify this card; it may be local fiat, cash, bank, e-wallet money, or stablecoin, but the supplied evidence does not establish the split.

### 6. Current Financial Behavior

**Market Evidence / Inference:** The defining financial behavior is a recurring remittance obligation fulfilled through a route that includes stablecoin. The Kansas City Fed estimates transfers at 29.3% of stablecoin activity, and Chainalysis identifies cross-border use in relevant regions, but neither source provides a clean panel of recurring family-money senders. Current rail choice, amount, cadence, and cash-out path are **Unknown**. [E04, E06–E09]

### 7. Current Crypto Behavior

**Market Evidence / Unknown:** The sender uses stablecoin somewhere in the remittance path and may buy, hold briefly, and forward it. The sender may not be a trader or long-term holder; broader crypto activity, token choice, custody, and stablecoin share of total remittances are **Unknown**. A transfer event alone does not establish a stablecoin money balance.

### 8. Stablecoin Motivation

**Market Evidence / Inference:** Plausible motivations are lower cost, faster movement, fewer access barriers, and reduced FX friction. One Chainalysis analysis estimated stablecoin remittance cost about 60% below fiat methods, but this is a market/provider comparison, not direct sender-reported motivation or proof of adoption. The relative importance of each motivation is **Unknown**. [E08–E09]

### 9. Pain Severity

**Inference / Unknown:** Remittance pain is **medium–high at category level** in costly, slow, or access-constrained corridors. The World Bank’s 6.36% global average remittance cost across 367 corridors is a benchmark for the conventional category, not a sender-level stablecoin pain score. Individual severity, corridor-specific cost, and willingness to switch are **Unknown**. [E10]

### 10. Frequency

**Inference / Unknown:** Recurring by definition, potentially aligned with a payroll cycle, household need, or other family obligation. Actual weekly/monthly cadence, amount, seasonality, number of recipients, and stablecoin use per transfer are **Unknown**. The card cannot be sized from aggregate transfer volume alone.

### 11. Economic / Relationship Value

**Hypothesis:** The sender’s family relationship can create high recurring relationship value, while lower time/cost/FX friction could create material economic value per transfer. Balance retention may be low if stablecoin is acquired and forwarded on the same day; that is a hypothesis, not a measured rate. A repeated sender relationship and a retained stablecoin balance are separate outcomes.

### 12. Current Alternatives

**Market Evidence / Inference:** Bank transfers, money-transfer operators, cash pickup, local e-wallets, bank/cash rails, multi-currency providers, and other conventional remittance routes are alternatives. The World Bank benchmark establishes the category’s cost baseline; it does not show which alternative this sender uses or whether stablecoin is cheaper in the sender’s corridor. Wise is an example of a scaled cross-border alternative, not a vendor recommendation. [E10, E12]

### 13. Why AIX

**Unknown / opportunity hypothesis:** No supplied evidence directly shows that AIX reaches recurring stablecoin family-money senders or delivers a materially differentiated sender outcome. The recurring family obligation could support a repeat relationship hypothesis, but the evidence does not establish AIX fit, sender preference, corridor reach, or retained balance.

### 14. Why NOT AIX

There is no direct AIX-specific sender evidence. The transfer may be fiat-native, stablecoin may be only a transient bridge, and strong conventional alternatives may already meet the sender’s needs. Global remittance volume, stablecoin transfer share, and a modeled cost comparison do not prove AIX demand. Corridor eligibility, trust, source/final money forms, and whether the sender values holding versus forwarding are all **Unknown**.

**Market Evidence / Unknown:** Sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America provide observed cross-border and FX-friction contexts, but they do not establish AIX channel access, country eligibility, sender trust, or willingness to switch. ### 17. Retention / Money Relationship

**Hypothesis / Unknown:** Repeated sending can create a high-value relationship even when the sender’s stablecoin balance is transient. Direct sender-side balance retention, time between acquisition and forwarding, repeat rate, and cross-period relationship are **Unknown**. No inference is made about recipient retention, because recipients are explicitly excluded from this card.

### 18. Evidence Strength

**Overall: Medium for the existence of a cross-border transfer/remittance Job and market-level stablecoin transfer activity; Low for recurring stablecoin family senders, user count, source/preferred forms, corridor economics, AIX reachability, and retention.** The strongest evidence is category-level remittance pain and transfer/cross-border market signals, not direct sender-level stablecoin adoption. [E04, E06–E11]

### Key Disconfirming Evidence

- The 29.3% transfer estimate includes trading, exchange, and other movement; it is not a remittance-sender share. [E04]
- World Bank remittance flows and cost benchmarks describe the generic category and conventional alternatives; they do not prove stablecoin adoption or AIX demand. [E10–E11]
- A sender may buy and forward stablecoin immediately, creating transaction recurrence without a retained money balance.
- The sender may prefer bank, cash, e-wallet, or other fiat rails, or stablecoin may not be part of the actual family-money path in the relevant corridor.
- Evidence about receiving or recipient-side payout cannot be projected onto the sender card.

### Validation Gate

**Status: NOT PASSED / candidate remains unranked.** Strategic validation requires:

1. Direct sender-level evidence that transfers are recurring, personal/family support and include a stablecoin step, with self-transfer, business settlement, and recipient-only behavior excluded.
2. Separate measurement of sender source currency, sender preferred form, acquisition/forwarding timing, amount, cadence, corridor pain, and current alternative.
3. Evidence distinguishing a recurring sender relationship from a retained stablecoin balance; no recipient retention conclusion should be substituted.
4. AIX-specific reachability and differentiated sender outcome in a bounded corridor. If stablecoin is absent, mostly just-in-time and immediately forwarded, or conventional rails solve the Job without residual pain, keep AIX fit **Unknown**.

## Round 2 bottom line

The three cards identify different money relationships and should not be collapsed into “stablecoin users”:

- S1 has the clearest evidence of recurring stablecoin receipt, but post-receipt preference and retained balances remain Unknown.
- S2 has the clearest intentional value-protection hypothesis in named high-inflation / FX-friction contexts, but cross-market scale and duration remain Unknown.
- S3 has meaningful category-level remittance pain and stablecoin transfer evidence, but recurring family-money **sender** adoption and sender retention remain Unknown.

No card is Primary. The minimum next evidence is direct, segment-specific, longitudinal user evidence plus AIX-specific reachability and differentiation; until then, AIX fit remains `Unknown / opportunity hypothesis` for all three.
