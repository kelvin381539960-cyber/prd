# Strategic Validation 14 — Round 2 / Evidence Cards C

> Research and access date: 2026-08-31
> Scope: S7 cross-border sole proprietor / small digital business stablecoin settlement user; S8 fiat-paid global worker / freelancer / international contractor; and three comparator controls.
> Disposition: S7 and S8 remain candidates under validation. This card makes no Primary or Secondary selection. The three comparators are not-target controls for this round, not permanent exclusions.

## 0. Scope and evidence discipline

This card answers one strategic question: which money relationships have enough independent evidence to justify further target-user validation, without treating a broad occupation, remittance category, or crypto identity as a target.

- **S7 is commercial settlement.** It is distinct from personal salary, family remittance, personal everyday spending, and trading/investment-only behavior.
- **S8 is explicitly fiat-paid.** Compensation arrives in fiat. A worker who receives compensation in stablecoin is not S8 even if the job, country, or client is otherwise similar.
- **Source Currency and Preferred Money Form are separate.** A fiat invoice or salary does not establish a stablecoin preference; USD-denominated income does not establish a stablecoin preference.
- **Market size is not additive.** Broad gig-worker, platform-worker, remittance, provider-volume, on-chain-volume, and stablecoin-balance numbers are not used as AIX TAM or reachable-user counts.
- **AIX fit is conditional and strategic.** No current AIX capability, feature, vendor, partner, implementation, page, API, or PRD conclusion is made here.
- **Evidence labels:** Fact = directly stated in a source or in the segment definition; Market Evidence = modeled, provider-reported, or network-level signal; Inference = bounded synthesis; Hypothesis = requires direct validation; Unknown = not established by the reviewed evidence.

The existing files used as local carryover are evidence/14-agent-2-stablecoin-users.md, evidence/14-round1-candidate-universe.md, and evidence/04-agent-g2-non-card-substitute-rails-evidence-card.md. Only one new external source was added in this round: the ILO report listed as R2-04 below. The other named sources are reused evidence, not new source expansion.

## 1. S7 — Cross-border sole proprietor / small digital business stablecoin settlement user

**Round 2 status:** Candidate under validation; no Primary or Secondary assignment.

### 1. Definition

**Research definition — Fact as a segment boundary; population Unknown.** A sole proprietor or small digital business that repeatedly uses stablecoin for cross-border commercial settlement: receiving client or customer revenue, settling invoices, paying contractors or suppliers, or moving business working capital. The defining job is commercial cash-flow settlement, not personal salary receipt, family support, personal everyday spend, or trading. This refines Round 1 C8 [R1-C8].

### 2. Jobs

**Inference / Unknown.** The jobs are to receive money from cross-border customers, settle business obligations, manage timing and FX friction, and keep operating funds usable between receipt and payment events. The reviewed public sources do not isolate this exact small-business cohort or directly rank these jobs.

### 3. Money Source

**Inference / Unknown.** The source is expected to be client invoices, business sales, or other commercial receipts. The 10,000-plus contractor payroll figure in the existing evidence cannot be relabeled as a small-business user count, and payroll evidence cannot be used as evidence of S7 money source [E14-2 C21; R1-C8].

### 4. Source Currency

**Unknown.** The pre-stablecoin currency or asset is not established for S7. It may be a local fiat, USD, EUR, GBP, another fiat invoice currency, or a crypto asset, but none can be selected from the current evidence. USD/EUR/GBP named in the payroll evidence describe employer funding for another cohort, not S7 [E14-2 C21].

### 5. Preferred Money Form

**Unknown / Hypothesis.** Stablecoin may be preferred for the settlement leg or as working capital, while operating expenses may end in local fiat or bank/e-wallet money. Immediate conversion is equally plausible. Use of stablecoin at one settlement event does not establish a preferred retained form.

### 6. Current Financial Behavior

**Inference / Market Evidence.** The hypothesized current pattern is recurring cross-border receivables and payables, with business funds moved between receipt and operating obligations. The Kansas City Fed transfer category and broader stablecoin payment estimates show that transfer and settlement activity exists, but they do not identify sole proprietors, business size, or the commercial share [E14-2 C07, C10-C13].

### 7. Current Crypto Behavior

**Fact as a segment boundary; prevalence and detail Unknown.** S7 uses stablecoin for commercial settlement by definition. It is Unknown whether the user holds it between events, converts immediately, uses other crypto before or after settlement, or maintains an intentional stablecoin working balance. The 29.3% transfer estimate is a balance/function model, not an S7 user count [E14-2 C07]. The Coins.ph QRPh evidence is an adjacent consumer-payment precedent, not evidence of S7 business settlement [G2].

### 8. Stablecoin Motivation

**Hypothesis / Unknown.** Plausible motivations are lower cross-border friction, more predictable settlement timing, access across banking boundaries, and a USD-like nominal form. These motivations are not directly observed in a representative S7 cohort. A remittance cost benchmark or a general stablecoin transfer share cannot prove S7 motivation.

### 9. Pain

**Inference / Unknown.** Potential pain includes fees, FX spreads, settlement delay, account or corridor availability, and difficulty paying distributed contractors or suppliers. The World Bank 6.36% average remittance-cost benchmark is a family-remittance alternative baseline, not a measurement of S7 pain or pricing [R2-03]. Business-specific severity and willingness to switch are Unknown.

### 10. Frequency

**Definition / Unknown.** The target definition requires repeated settlement, normally linked to invoices, client receipts, contractor payments, or supplier obligations. Actual cadence, number of counterparties, amount, seasonality, and stablecoin share of business flow are Unknown.

### 11. Economic-Relationship Value

**Hypothesis.** Potential relationship value is high relative to an episodic consumer payment because business cash flow can recur and connect receipts with multiple obligations. The value is not established: it falls materially if the user converts immediately, uses stablecoin only once, or keeps the relationship with an existing fiat provider. No revenue, balance, or margin estimate is inferred.

### 12. Alternatives

**Strong Market Evidence for alternatives; S7 switching behavior Unknown.** Alternatives include bank wires and local bank accounts, ordinary business payment accounts, payment platforms, Wise, Revolut, local e-wallets, and fiat settlement through a client or marketplace. Wise reports 18.9 million active customers, 243.5 billion dollars of cross-border volume, 39 billion dollars of customer holdings, 43.6 billion dollars of card spend, and 75% of Q4 payments under 20 seconds; these are provider-level alternative signals, not S7 adoption or market share [R2-01]. Revolut reports a large active retail relationship and primary-bank usage, likewise showing an established fiat alternative rather than stablecoin substitution [R2-02].

### 13. Why AIX

**Conditional strategic fit — Hypothesis.** S7 has a clear cross-border commercial job and could, if independently validated, create a recurring money relationship around business receipts and obligations. It is therefore a reasonable candidate for targeted discovery. This is not a claim that AIX currently serves S7 or that S7 should be selected as a target.

### 14. Why NOT AIX

**Evidence gap / boundary.** There is no public evidence in the reviewed set that isolates small digital businesses using stablecoin, proves their preferred retained form, or shows that stablecoin creates more value than Wise, Revolut, banks, payment platforms, or local rails. Business and personal money may be mixed for a sole proprietor, and the required separation is not evidenced. The candidate must not be upgraded because stablecoin transfers, B2B volume, contractor payroll, or a broad digital-worker category exists.

### 15. Market Size — category-relevant / reachable / unknown

- **Category-relevant:** **Unknown.** ILO evidence supports a broad digital-labour and independent-contractor context, but it spans online and location-based platforms and is not a count of small businesses or stablecoin users [R2-04]. Existing stablecoin transfer and payment estimates do not isolate S7 [E14-2 C07, C10-C13].
- **Reachable:** **Unknown.** No reviewed source provides a segment-specific identifier, cohort count, or reachable distribution base for cross-border sole proprietors and small digital businesses.
- **Stablecoin-active subset:** **Unknown.** No reliable count or penetration rate is available.
- **TAM rule:** Do not use broad gig-worker, B2B, B2C, total stablecoin-transfer, provider-volume, or remittance figures as AIX TAM. The category is a context, not a sized target.

### 16. Reachability

**Unknown.** The relevant user is defined by a combination of business size, cross-border cash flow, stablecoin settlement, and repeated behavior. Generic platform-worker totals, client counts, or provider customers cannot be treated as reachable S7 users. Reachability requires a separately identified cohort; no channel or provider selection is made in this card.

### 17. Retention

**Hypothesis / Unknown.** Working capital may remain in stablecoin between receipt and obligation events, which would create a durable relationship. Immediate conversion for payroll, suppliers, tax, or local expenses is equally plausible. No longitudinal user or balance panel measures S7 retention, and a transfer event is not retention [E14-2 C07, C21].

### 18. Evidence Strength + Disconfirming Evidence + Validation Gate

- **Evidence strength:** **Low** for S7-specific population, source currency, preferred form, reachability, and retention; **Medium** for the existence of adjacent cross-border transfer and commercial-settlement contexts. ILO is useful for boundary and measurement caution, not for stablecoin adoption [R2-04].
- **Disconfirming evidence:** Direct cohort evidence showing that small digital businesses overwhelmingly use fiat alternatives, convert stablecoin immediately, use it only for isolated transactions, or do not experience incremental value would disconfirm the durable-S7 hypothesis. Evidence that the observed commercial flow is mostly institutional, exchange, internal, or unrelated to sole proprietors would also disconfirm S7 sizing.
- **Validation Gate:** Keep S7 as a candidate only until anonymized cohort evidence identifies the business role, repeated commercial settlement, pre-stablecoin source currency, preferred final form, alternative used, and whether stablecoin is intentionally retained or reused across periods. If those conditions are not observed, do not upgrade S7 to a target or use it for TAM.

## 2. S8 — Fiat-paid global worker / freelancer / international contractor

**Round 2 status:** Candidate under validation; no Primary or Secondary assignment.

### 1. Definition

**Research definition — Fact as a segment boundary; population and stablecoin fit Unknown.** An individual who receives employment, freelance, or contractor compensation in fiat from a foreign employer, client, or platform and manages that money across borders. **S8 is not stablecoin-paid.** A person receiving compensation in stablecoin belongs to a separate stablecoin-paid cohort even if the job and geography match [R1-C1, R1-C2].

### 2. Jobs

**Fact / Inference / Unknown.** The jobs are to receive compensation reliably, convert or hold it in a usable form, move it across borders, pay expenses, and save or manage timing. The exact mix of employment, freelance, marketplace, and international-contractor jobs is Unknown.

### 3. Money Source

**Fact by definition; worker-level details Unknown.** Employer, client, or platform compensation paid in fiat. This field must not be rewritten as stablecoin income and must not be merged with the stablecoin-paid cohort.

### 4. Source Currency

**Fiat by definition; exact currency Unknown.** The source may be USD, EUR, GBP, or another local/foreign fiat currency, but the worker-level split and corridor are not established. Employer funding currencies named in a stablecoin-payroll case do not prove S8 preference or behavior [E14-2 C21; R1-C2].

### 5. Preferred Money Form

**Market Evidence / Unknown.** The available alternative forms are bank money, multi-currency balances, local fiat, e-wallet money, and card-linked balances. Wise and Revolut evidence shows mature fiat choices, but it does not reveal the preferred form of S8 or establish a stablecoin preference. Stablecoin as a retained form is Unknown [R2-01, R2-02].

### 6. Current Financial Behavior

**Inference / Market Evidence.** The likely behavior is recurring fiat payroll or invoice receipt followed by conversion, cross-border transfer, saving, or spending. Wise provides a large-scale cross-border alternative with 18.9 million active customers, 243.5 billion dollars in cross-border volume, customer holdings, card spend, and reported fast payments; Revolut provides another large active retail and primary-bank alternative. These sources establish a well-served job, not S8-specific usage [R2-01, R2-02].

### 7. Current Crypto Behavior

**Unknown.** S8 does not receive compensation in stablecoin by definition. The worker may separately own crypto, use stablecoin, or use neither; no reviewed source identifies that behavior for fiat-paid global workers. Crypto ownership, high crypto adoption, or access to a USD income stream must not be treated as evidence of stablecoin use.

### 8. Stablecoin Motivation

**Unknown / Hypothesis.** Possible motivations include a deliberate cross-border bridge, speed, lower perceived friction, or a dollar-like balance, but none is directly linked to the fiat-paid cohort. A stated preference for USD, foreign currency, or predictable nominal value does not prove a preference for stablecoin [R1-C2]. The Coins.ph payment precedent shows an adjacent use case, not S8 demand [G2].

### 9. Pain

**Inference / Unknown.** Cross-border workers may face FX cost, transfer delay, account availability, and payment timing friction. Existing fiat alternatives appear to address much of the job. The World Bank remittance-cost benchmark is not a worker-payment pain estimate, so it is only an adjacent context and cannot size or qualify S8 [R2-03].

### 10. Frequency

**Definition / Unknown.** Compensation is recurring by the segment definition, tied to payroll or invoice cycles. Actual cadence, amount, cross-border event frequency, and stablecoin-use frequency are Unknown. Repeated fiat income is not repeated stablecoin behavior.

### 11. Economic-Relationship Value

**Hypothesis / Unknown.** The underlying income relationship can be recurring and economically important. The incremental value of a stablecoin relationship is unproven because the worker may already be well served by fiat accounts and cross-border providers, and may convert immediately. Recurring income alone is not evidence of stablecoin retention.

### 12. Alternatives

**Strong Market Evidence.** Alternatives include employer or marketplace payroll, bank transfers, local bank accounts, e-wallets, ordinary cards, Wise, Revolut, and other fiat multi-currency services. Wise’s active-customer, cross-border-volume, holdings, card-spend, and speed claims demonstrate scale in the alternative category; Revolut’s annual-report evidence demonstrates a large active retail and primary-bank relationship [R2-01, R2-02]. These are alternatives, not proof that S8 users use or prefer them.

### 13. Why AIX

**Conditional strategic fit — Hypothesis.** S8 could be relevant only as a narrowly defined validation audience if a fiat-paid worker independently reports a recurring stablecoin job and incremental value beyond existing fiat alternatives. The strategic opportunity, if any, would be the money relationship around cross-border work income, not the broad identity of being a freelancer or international worker.

### 14. Why NOT AIX

**Evidence gap / boundary.** The current evidence strongly supports the existence of the fiat-paid cross-border job and strong alternatives, but it does not support stablecoin awareness, use, preference, switching intent, or retention in this cohort. S8 must not be upgraded because income is in USD, work is international, or the person is a freelancer. If stablecoin behavior is not independently observed, S8 remains an adjacent research candidate or comparator rather than a target.

### 15. Market Size — category-relevant / reachable / unknown

- **Category-relevant:** **Unknown for stablecoin fit.** ILO establishes that digital labour platforms cover heterogeneous online and location-based work and that the platform-mediated workforce is difficult to estimate; its survey and platform counts are not a current count of fiat-paid global workers or stablecoin users [R2-04].
- **Reachable:** **Unknown.** Wise and Revolut customer totals are provider-level populations, not S8 counts. ILO survey scope is research evidence, not a reachable-user base.
- **Stablecoin-active subset:** **Unknown.** No source provides a clean fiat-paid worker denominator, stablecoin adoption rate, or retained-balance rate.
- **TAM rule:** Do not use broad online-gig-worker ranges, all freelancers, all remittance flows, or USD income as AIX TAM. No broad gig-worker number from the prior brief is imported into this card.

### 16. Reachability

**Unknown.** “Global worker” is not a sufficiently specific reachable cohort. A valid reachability estimate would need to identify fiat-paid status, cross-border money need, and independent stablecoin behavior. Existing provider customer numbers and generic platform-worker estimates cannot substitute for that evidence.

### 17. Retention

**Hypothesis / Unknown.** Recurring fiat compensation could create a repeat relationship with a financial provider, but stablecoin-specific retention is unproven. The worker may cash out or spend after each fiat receipt, may never use stablecoin, or may retain stablecoin only as a separate asset. Balance retention must not be inferred from income recurrence [E14-2 C02, C21].

### 18. Evidence Strength + Disconfirming Evidence + Validation Gate

- **Evidence strength:** **Strong** for the existence of the fiat-paid cross-border job and mature fiat alternatives; **Low** for current stablecoin behavior, preference, reachability, incremental value, and retention. The ILO evidence strengthens the category boundary and measurement caution, not stablecoin demand [R2-04].
- **Disconfirming evidence:** A direct fiat-paid cohort showing little or no stablecoin use, no incremental value over existing fiat alternatives, immediate conversion when stablecoin is tried, or no repeated stablecoin behavior would disconfirm S8 as a stablecoin target. USD preference without stablecoin use is disconfirming, not supporting, evidence.
- **Validation Gate:** Keep S8 as a candidate only if anonymized evidence confirms fiat-paid status and a repeated, intentional stablecoin job with incremental value versus the worker’s current alternatives, including the final money form and cross-period retention or reuse. Otherwise keep S8 outside the target set.

## 3. Comparator controls

These controls are deliberately not upgraded into S7 or S8. Each has a real money job, but the reviewed evidence does not establish the specific stablecoin relationship required for a target conclusion.

### Comparator 1 — Generic international consumer / digital nomad

- **Definition:** A person who travels, lives, or spends across borders and uses ordinary bank, card, cash, e-wallet, or multi-currency money. Work income, commercial settlement, and stablecoin behavior are not part of the definition.
- **Why it cannot be directly upgraded to target:** International mobility is a demographic or lifestyle label, not a stablecoin job. It does not establish business cash flow, fiat-paid work, source currency, or stablecoin preference; it is also too broad to be a behaviorally coherent target.
- **Alternatives:** Bank accounts, ordinary cards, cash, local e-wallets, and mature multi-currency/cross-border providers such as Wise and Revolut. Their reported scale, holdings, card spend, speed, and primary-bank relationships show strong alternatives [R2-01, R2-02].
- **Stablecoin fit:** **Unknown / Low evidence.** Travel or international spending can occur entirely in fiat. Coins.ph demonstrates an adjacent stablecoin-payment precedent in the Philippines, but not digital-nomad adoption or retention [G2].
- **Retention:** A fiat provider or bank relationship may recur as travel and spending recur; stablecoin balance retention is Unknown. Episodic travel usage is not a durable stablecoin money relationship.
- **Evidence and strength:** **Strong** for the existence of cross-border consumer alternatives; **Low** for stablecoin use, stablecoin preference, and a digital-nomad denominator. Provider totals are not this comparator’s population.
- **Upgrade gate:** Only reconsider if a separately identified cohort shows a repeated stablecoin job and incremental value beyond fiat alternatives. Travel frequency, card spend, or USD access alone is insufficient.

### Comparator 2 — Generic OFW / remittance fiat user

- **Definition:** An overseas worker or household sender/recipient who uses conventional fiat, bank, cash, e-wallet, or money-transfer rails for family or personal support. Sender and recipient remain different jobs even within this comparator.
- **Why it cannot be directly upgraded to target:** Remittance pain and category size do not prove stablecoin behavior. A sender may want a reliable fiat delivery route, while a recipient may want local cash or e-wallet money. Neither direction establishes a stablecoin starting pool, preferred form, or retained balance.
- **Alternatives:** Bank transfer, money-transfer operator, cash pickup, local bank, local e-wallet, and other fiat remittance rails. The World Bank reports a 6.36% global average remittance cost across 367 corridors; this is an alternative-rail benchmark, not stablecoin adoption or AIX TAM [R2-03].
- **Stablecoin fit:** **Unknown.** Stablecoin could be a transfer bridge in some corridors, but no evidence in this generic fiat comparator shows that the sender or recipient chooses it. A transfer volume or remittance total cannot be projected onto stablecoin use.
- **Retention:** **Unknown and likely event-dependent.** The recipient may end with local fiat, cash, or e-wallet money; the sender may repeat the fiat route. Stablecoin retention is not established, and recurring remittance is not recurring stablecoin balance.
- **Evidence and strength:** **Strong** for the existence of a large, costly alternative category; **Low** for stablecoin fit, final form, and retention. World Bank benchmarks are useful context only [R2-03].
- **Upgrade gate:** Reconsider only after a defined cohort independently demonstrates a stablecoin event, source and final money forms, repeat behavior, and incremental value versus the conventional rail. No remittance corridor or capability is prioritized in this card.

### Comparator 3 — Crypto trader / investor-only

- **Definition:** A person using stablecoin for trading quotation, settlement, liquidity, portfolio allocation, or yield, without evidenced commercial settlement, fiat-paid work, family remittance, or real-life spending as the primary job.
- **Why it cannot be directly upgraded to target:** Stablecoin fit may be high for capital or trading purposes, but the job is not S7 commercial cash flow or S8 fiat-paid work. A large stablecoin balance, exchange activity, DeFi activity, or long holding period does not prove a real-life money relationship.
- **Alternatives:** Exchange fiat balances, bank money, other crypto assets, portfolio instruments, and other trading or investment balances. The alternatives are role-dependent; no particular provider or implementation is selected here.
- **Stablecoin fit:** **High for the defined trading/investment job; Unknown for S7/S8.** The Kansas City Fed estimates 48.8% of stablecoin activity associated with trading assets, 21.2% idle, 29.3% transfers, and 0.7% payments. These are modeled balance/function shares, not trader counts or evidence of commercial settlement [R2-05; E14-2 C07-C08].
- **Retention:** A portfolio or tactical liquidity balance may persist, but that is asset retention, not retention of an operating-money relationship. Duration, unique users, and the share that later becomes business or worker money are Unknown.
- **Evidence and strength:** **Strong** for trading/non-payment being major market functions; **Low** for unique consumer counts, S7/S8 overlap, and real-life retention. Philadelphia Fed and Visa methodology also caution that DeFi, retail-sized transfers, and card activity do not map cleanly to consumer money roles [E14-2 C09, C15].
- **Upgrade gate:** Only reclassify a person or cohort after evidence identifies a separate commercial or fiat-paid work job, source currency, repeated real-life settlement behavior, and an intentional operating-money relationship. Otherwise retain as a non-target comparator.

## 4. Cross-card conclusion

1. **S7:** commercially meaningful relationship hypothesis, but the small-business population, source currency, stablecoin preference, reachability, and retention are Low/Unknown. No tier selection.
2. **S8:** strong evidence for the fiat-paid cross-border job and alternatives, but stablecoin fit is Low/Unknown. It is not stablecoin-paid, and USD income cannot be used as a proxy for stablecoin preference. No tier selection.
3. **Comparators:** international mobility, generic fiat remittance, and crypto trading/investment each describe real jobs, but none can be directly upgraded into S7/S8 without independent stablecoin evidence.
4. **No TAM conclusion:** no broad gig-worker, remittance, provider, on-chain, balance, or payment number is converted into AIX TAM or reachable users.
5. **No downstream prioritization:** this card does not select Primary, prioritize Remittance/QR/Send, or make any feature, vendor, partner, implementation, or PRD recommendation.
