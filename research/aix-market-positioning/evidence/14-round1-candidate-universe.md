# Strategic Validation 14 — Round 1 Candidate User Universe

> **Round 1 materialization — candidate pool only.** This document does not select a Primary or Secondary group, rank candidates, assign AIX capability priority, or make a product decision. It uses only the controller-verified evidence supplied in the task brief; no network search was performed.

## 1. Scope and reading rules

The unit of analysis is a repeated money behavior and its underlying job, not a nationality, occupation label, region, or crypto identity. A person or small business can exhibit more than one behavior over time. The eight candidates below are therefore a research universe, not mutually exclusive people counts and not an additive TAM.

The following distinctions are hard boundaries in this round:

- **Stablecoin-paid** means compensation is received in stablecoin. It is not the same as preferring USD or receiving fiat payroll.
- **Fiat-paid** means compensation is received in fiat. It is kept separate from stablecoin-paid even when the worker has a cross-border job.
- **Sender** and **recipient** are separate because the source of funds, job, final money form, and retention behavior differ.
- **Holder/saver**, **trader**, **everyday spender**, and **crypto-gains-to-spend converter** are separate. Holding or trading does not prove everyday spending.
- **Source Currency** means the currency or asset before the stablecoin step. **Preferred Money Form** means the form the person wants to keep or use after receiving, converting, or transferring funds. One must not be inferred from the other.
- A retail-sized transfer is not automatically a retail payment. Card volume proves spend activity, but not a durable stablecoin balance. [S02–S03]

Evidence labels used below: **Fact** = directly stated in the supplied evidence; **Market Evidence** = modeled, provider-reported, or market-level evidence; **Inference** = bounded synthesis; **Hypothesis** = requires direct validation; **Unknown** = the supplied evidence cannot establish it.

## 3. Final Candidate Pool

### C1. Recurring stablecoin-paid global earners

**Behavioral Definition**

An individual repeatedly receives compensation for work—employment, contracting, creator work, or a digital/Web3 service—in stablecoin. The defining event is stablecoin-paid compensation, not merely having crypto or preferring USD.

**Core Jobs**

Receive cross-border compensation; access a portable dollar-like form; decide whether to hold, transfer, cash out, or spend each pay event.

**Money Source**

**Fact:** Employer, client, or platform compensation. The supplied Deel/BVNK evidence concerns contractors opting into stablecoin payroll. It does not establish that all “stablecoin income” in the survey is salary or work compensation. [S04–S06]

**Source Currency**

**Fact / Unknown:** USD, EUR, and GBP are explicitly named as employer funding currencies in the Deel/BVNK payroll arrangement. The worker-level local source-currency mix and any other source currencies are Unknown. [S05]

**Preferred Money Form**

**Inference / Unknown:** Stablecoin is preferred at the receiving step by the opted-in worker. The preferred form after receipt—stablecoin balance, local fiat, bank/e-wallet money, or card-linked balance—is not established. USD preference in high-inflation markets must not be rewritten as stablecoin preference. [S06]

**Current Behavior**

**Market Evidence / User Evidence:** More than 10,000 contractors across more than 100 countries reportedly opted into stablecoin payroll. In the BVNK/YouGov survey of 4,658 adults across 15 countries, the crypto-engaged sample reported 39% stablecoin income, and the reported stablecoin-income cohort said it represented approximately 35% of annual income. The sample is not general population, and the survey category is broader than salary. [S04–S05]

**Stablecoin Role**

Compensation receiving rail and portable digital-dollar form; it may then become savings, a transfer input, a payment input, or an immediate cash-out. The evidence does not establish which downstream role dominates.

**Frequency**

**Inference / Unknown:** Recurring by segment definition, normally tied to payroll or invoice cycles. Actual cadence, amount, and share of total earnings are Unknown beyond the reported survey statistic.

**Alternatives**

**Market Evidence / Inference:** Bank payroll, ordinary payroll/payment platforms, bank transfer, remittance rails, Wise, and Revolut are plausible alternatives. Wise reports 18.9m active customers and $243.5B cross-border volume; Revolut reports a large active retail and primary-bank relationship. These prove strong alternatives, not stablecoin substitution. [S09–S11]

**Market Evidence**

The provider-reported contractor opt-in base and the crypto-engaged survey establish a real receiving behavior. Deel’s 2026 hiring evidence also connects high-inflation markets with greater contractor preference for USD/stablecoin, with Argentina significant, but the same evidence explicitly does not equate USD preference with stablecoin preference. [S04–S06]

**Retention Hypothesis**

**Hypothesis:** Recurring pay events could create a repeat money relationship and a balance between pay cycles. Retention may be weak where the worker converts or transfers immediately; the 28% near-term convert/spend signal for surveyed holders is consistent with that possibility but is not payroll-specific. [S04]

**Key Counter-evidence**

The survey is crypto-engaged rather than general population; “stablecoin income” is not a clean salary denominator; USD preference is not stablecoin preference; and quick conversion or spending can produce repeated receipts without a retained balance. [S04–S06]

**Evidence Strength**

**Medium** for the existence of a recurring stablecoin-paid cohort; **Low–Medium** for prevalence, stablecoin share of income, durable balance retention, and worker-level source-currency mix.

**Unknowns**

Salary versus freelance versus creator/platform mix; country and corridor mix; worker-level source and final currencies; cash-out rate; balance duration; stablecoin share of household spending; willingness to switch from existing payroll/transfer rails.

### C2. Fiat-paid global earners with cross-border money needs

**Behavioral Definition**

An individual earns compensation in fiat and repeatedly needs to receive, convert, hold, move, or spend that income across borders. Stablecoin use is not assumed; the candidate is defined by the job and current fiat-paid relationship.

**Core Jobs**

Get paid reliably; manage cross-border conversion and timing; retain usable value; send or spend money internationally with low friction.

**Money Source**

**Fact / Inference:** Employer, client, or platform compensation paid in fiat. The specific employment or freelance mix is Unknown.

**Source Currency**

**Fact / Unknown:** Fiat by definition for this candidate; the exact currency mix and corridor are Unknown. The supplied evidence names USD/EUR/GBP in a payroll context but does not establish that mix for all fiat-paid global earners. [S05–S06]

**Preferred Money Form**

**Market Evidence / Inference:** A fiat multi-currency balance, bank account, e-wallet, card-linked balance, or local fiat is the currently evidenced alternative family. Stablecoin as a preferred held form is **Unknown**, not implied by cross-border work.

**Current Behavior**

**Market Evidence:** Wise reports 18.9m active customers, $243.5B cross-border volume, $39B customer holdings, $43.6B card spend, and 75% of Q4 payments under 20 seconds. Revolut reports large-scale active retail and primary-bank relationships. These indicate that a fiat-paid global worker can already use mature alternatives. [S10–S11]

**Stablecoin Role**

**Hypothesis / Unknown:** Possible cross-border bridge or optional dollar-like balance, but no supplied evidence proves that this fiat-paid cohort currently prefers, receives, holds, or spends stablecoin.

**Frequency**

**Inference / Unknown:** Potentially recurring per payroll or invoice cycle; actual payment cadence and cross-border event frequency are Unknown.

**Alternatives**

**Strong Market Evidence:** Wise and Revolut are direct alternative families for cross-border receiving, holding, conversion, and spending. Bank transfers, cards, local accounts, and e-wallets remain plausible additional alternatives. [S10–S11]

**Market Evidence**

The scale, holdings, card-spend, speed, and primary-bank evidence for Wise/Revolut show a large and well-served fiat cross-border job. Deel’s high-inflation hiring evidence shows more interest in USD/stablecoin among some contractors, but does not establish stablecoin preference or switching in this candidate. [S06, S10–S11]

**Retention Hypothesis**

**Hypothesis / Unknown:** The worker may retain a balance with the existing fiat provider because the relationship is recurring. Stablecoin-specific retention is unproven and should not be inferred from the size of the cross-border worker market.

**Key Counter-evidence**

Wise and Revolut already have large active relationships and fast cross-border payment performance. The World Bank’s wide online-gig-worker estimate is explicitly not a reachable-target count, and ILO notes that active platform-worker totals are hard to estimate. [S10–S11, S15]

**Evidence Strength**

**Strong** for the existence of the underlying cross-border fiat job and alternatives; **Low** for current stablecoin behavior or stablecoin preference.

**Unknowns**

Stablecoin awareness and adoption; switching intent; source-currency mix; pay frequency; retention with current provider; stablecoin share of income/spend; corridor-specific friction; reachable cohort size.

### C3. FX-friction savers converting earnings or savings into digital dollars

**Behavioral Definition**

An individual converts or holds stablecoin primarily to protect the nominal value of paychecks or savings against inflation, devaluation, FX friction, or restricted access to dollar-like money. The primary job is value protection, not trading, yield, or routine spending.

**Core Jobs**

Preserve nominal value; obtain USD-like exposure/access; reduce exposure to local currency deterioration; decide when to convert back for spending or other obligations.

**Money Source**

**User Evidence / Inference:** Local-currency earnings and savings are evidenced in the Argentina use case. Other source-of-funds paths are Unknown. [S07]

**Source Currency**

**User Evidence / Inference:** ARS/local currency is the explicit Argentina context. The source-currency mix in other high-inflation or FX-friction markets is Unknown. [S07–S08]

**Preferred Money Form**

**User Evidence:** USDT/USDC are named together as the digital-dollar form used in the Argentina paycheck/savings-protection context. This supports a use-case-specific stablecoin preference, not a universal token or global form preference. [S07]

**Current Behavior**

**Market Evidence / User Evidence:** Argentina evidence describes stablecoin use for paycheck and savings protection. Sub-Saharan Africa evidence identifies stablecoins in cross-border and FX/inflation-protection contexts. The Kansas City Fed’s 21.2% idle estimate can include small savings or forgotten balances, but it is not a saver cohort or a retention measure. [S01, S07–S08]

**Stablecoin Role**

Digital store of value and dollar-substitution form. It may later be transferred or spent, but those downstream events are not the defining behavior.

**Frequency**

**Inference / Unknown:** Conversion may recur when income arrives or FX risk rises; holding is intended to persist across events. Actual conversion cadence, balance size, and holding duration are Unknown.

**Alternatives**

**Inference / Unknown:** Physical USD, bank-held USD, formal FX access, and other local savings forms are plausible alternatives. Their relative use is not measured in the supplied evidence.

**Market Evidence**

Chainalysis provides the strongest context-specific evidence for Argentina and Sub-Saharan Africa. The 21.2% idle balance-function estimate is only directional because idle can include rarely used, forgotten, or small savings balances. [S01, S07–S08]

**Retention Hypothesis**

**Hypothesis:** This has a plausible intentional balance relationship because the job is to preserve value rather than immediately route funds. The evidence does not provide longitudinal retention, duration, or proof that a saver keeps stablecoin instead of converting to cash or another dollar form.

**Key Counter-evidence**

Nigeria’s 2025 centralized-exchange fiat-purchase mix was approximately 89% BTC and 7% USDT. Therefore high inflation or crypto activity cannot be used as a shortcut to infer stablecoin preference. Also, 48.8% of modeled stablecoin activity was associated with trading assets, so “holding stablecoin” is not automatically saving. [S01, S08]

**Evidence Strength**

**Medium–Strong** for the existence of the behavior in named market contexts; **Low** for cross-market scale, duration, household penetration, and token-level share.

**Unknowns**

Share of stablecoin users whose primary job is savings; holding duration; balance persistence; household-income share; stablecoin versus physical/bank USD choice; source-currency mix outside Argentina; spend and transfer frequency; willingness to use a new provider.

### C4. Recurring stablecoin cross-border senders

**Behavioral Definition**

An individual repeatedly initiates a personal or family-related cross-border transfer where stablecoin is part of the transfer path. The sender may be moving own funds or supporting another household; the supplied evidence does not cleanly separate those cases.

**Core Jobs**

Move value across borders; support a person or household; reduce time, cost, FX, or access friction; ensure the recipient can reach a usable local money form.

**Money Source**

**Inference / Unknown:** Sender wages, savings, or other personal income are plausible. The supplied evidence does not provide a person-level source-of-funds split.

**Source Currency**

**Unknown:** Local fiat, USD, or another currency/asset may precede the stablecoin step. Stablecoin is treated as a transfer instrument, not assumed to be the sender’s source currency.

**Preferred Money Form**

**Inference / Unknown:** The sender may prefer a stablecoin transfer bridge while the recipient may prefer local fiat, cash, bank, or e-wallet value. The sender’s final preferred form and the recipient’s actual payout form are not measured.

**Current Behavior**

**Market Evidence:** The Kansas City Fed estimates 29.3% of stablecoin activity as transfers. Chainalysis identifies stablecoin cross-border use in Sub-Saharan Africa. The World Bank’s 6.36% average remittance cost across 367 corridors establishes a pain benchmark, not stablecoin adoption. [S01, S08–S09]

**Stablecoin Role**

Cross-border transfer bridge or settlement asset; it may be bought and forwarded quickly rather than held as the sender’s money balance.

**Frequency**

**Inference / Unknown:** Recurring by candidate definition; actual weekly/monthly cadence, amount, and corridor distribution are Unknown.

**Alternatives**

Bank transfers, money-transfer operators, cash pickup, e-wallets, and other local remittance rails. The World Bank cost benchmark describes the problem category but does not identify which alternative a sender uses. [S09]

**Market Evidence**

Transfer is materially larger than the modeled payment share in the Kansas City Fed estimate, and Sub-Saharan Africa evidence supports a cross-border use case. World Bank remittance flows of approximately $685B to LMICs and $40B to the Philippines show category size only; they are not AIX TAM or stablecoin-user counts. [S01, S08, S14]

**Retention Hypothesis**

**Hypothesis:** Repeat sending can create a recurring relationship, but stablecoin balance retention may be low if the sender acquires and forwards on the same day. A balance that remains for value protection would be a different or overlapping C3 behavior.

**Key Counter-evidence**

The 29.3% transfer bucket includes non-remittance transfers, including trading or other movement. Remittance cost pain and global flow size do not prove stablecoin adoption, stablecoin preference, or sender retention. [S01, S09, S14]

**Evidence Strength**

**Medium** for a real cross-border transfer job and market activity; **Low** for sender penetration, stablecoin share, frequency, corridor economics, and retained balances.

**Unknowns**

Family support versus self-transfer share; source currency; stablecoin acquisition path; local payout form; cadence and amount; sender retention; recipient retention; stablecoin share of total remittance flows; corridor-level switching behavior.

### C5. Recurring stablecoin cross-border recipients

**Behavioral Definition**

An individual or household repeatedly receives personal or family-related cross-border funds through a transfer path that uses stablecoin. The recipient is distinct from the sender even when they belong to the same transfer relationship.

**Core Jobs**

Receive household support; access usable local money; manage timing and FX risk; optionally retain part of the received value.

**Money Source**

**Fact / Inference:** The incoming money is sender-funded personal or family support, not the recipient’s salary by default. The sender’s underlying income and the recipient’s household sources are Unknown.

**Source Currency**

**Unknown:** The sender’s local fiat, USD, or other source currency is not identified. Stablecoin may be an intermediate form, while the recipient’s final currency is a separate question.

**Preferred Money Form**

**Inference / Unknown:** Local fiat, cash, bank, or e-wallet money may be preferred for household use; a recipient who deliberately retains stablecoin may also overlap with C3. The supplied evidence does not establish the final form or split.

**Current Behavior**

**Market Evidence:** Transfer activity is estimated at 29.3% of stablecoin activity, and Sub-Saharan Africa evidence identifies cross-border utility. The 6.36% global average remittance-cost benchmark and LMIC/Philippines flow figures establish the size and pain of the generic category, not stablecoin recipient adoption. [S01, S08–S09, S14]

**Stablecoin Role**

Inbound transfer bridge, temporary balance, or—if intentionally retained—an additional store-of-value form. The recipient role must not be treated as a payment or salary role.

**Frequency**

**Inference / Unknown:** Recurring by candidate definition; actual receipt cadence, amount, seasonality, and number of senders are Unknown.

**Alternatives**

Bank transfer, money-transfer operator, cash pickup, e-wallet, and local cash/bank rails. The supplied World Bank evidence is an average cost benchmark, not a recipient preference survey. [S09]

**Market Evidence**

The evidence supports cross-border transfer as a material stablecoin function and a meaningful remittance pain category. It does not isolate recipients, reveal recipient-level stablecoin use, or show that the recipient retains funds after receipt. [S01, S08–S09, S14]

**Retention Hypothesis**

**Hypothesis:** Repeat receipt can create a recurring money relationship, but immediate local cash-out is plausible. If recipients retain stablecoin for value protection, that is a separate retention mode and should be tested as C3 rather than assumed for all recipients.

**Key Counter-evidence**

Transfer share mixes remittance with other transfers; the World Bank category-size figures do not prove stablecoin adoption; and a recipient can receive stablecoin in the route while ending with local fiat or cash. [S01, S09, S14]

**Evidence Strength**

**Low–Medium** for the existence of the recipient-side job; **Low** for stablecoin-specific penetration, final-form preference, frequency, and retention.

**Unknowns**

Recipient country and corridor; sender relationship; final payout form; immediate conversion rate; retained balance; source and destination currency; household-spend share; stablecoin awareness and willingness to receive it.

### C6. Stablecoin-balance everyday spenders

**Behavioral Definition**

An individual repeatedly uses an intentionally available stablecoin balance for routine personal purchases or obligations. This is stricter than owning a crypto card, making one card purchase, or spending crypto that is converted to local fiat at the point of sale.

**Core Jobs**

Turn an active balance into routine purchasing power; pay goods/services and recurring obligations; reduce payment friction; keep a usable money balance between spending events.

**Money Source**

**Unknown:** Possible sources include stablecoin-paid income, savings, transfer receipts, or converted crypto proceeds. The supplied evidence does not provide a clean source split for everyday spenders.

**Source Currency**

**Unknown:** The upstream source may be local fiat, USD/EUR/GBP compensation, or crypto proceeds. Visa’s point-of-sale conversion capability describes the spend rail, not the consumer’s original source currency. [S02]

**Preferred Money Form**

**Inference:** An intentionally maintained stablecoin balance is the defining active form for this candidate; the merchant may receive local fiat after conversion. A card-linked balance or immediate POS conversion without retained stablecoin belongs in C7 or an occasional-payment comparator unless repeat balance behavior is proven.

**Current Behavior**

**User Evidence / Market Evidence:** In the BVNK/YouGov survey of 4,658 adults across 15 countries, the crypto-engaged sample reported 27% everyday stablecoin activity, 71% likely use of a linked card, and 28% of holders converting or spending within days. The same evidence reports lower fees, security, and global access as motivations. Visa reports recovery in 2025 crypto-linked card volume and a leading APAC issuer share. These signals prove interest or spend activity, not durable stablecoin balances. [S02, S04]

**Stablecoin Role**

Active money balance and/or funding input for routine spend. Depending on the rail, stablecoin can be converted into local fiat at the point of sale; the role must therefore be recorded separately from the final merchant settlement form. [S02]

**Frequency**

**Inference / Unknown:** Routine and recurring by definition; the survey does not establish a frequency threshold, spend share, average balance, or country-level rate.

**Alternatives**

KAST’s generic stablecoin everyday-account proposition and RedotPay’s crypto-card/stablecoin-spend proposition are strong market alternatives. Wise, Revolut, local bank/e-wallet balances, and ordinary cards are additional substitutes for the purchasing-power job. [S10–S13]

**Market Evidence**

KAST reports more than 1m users and approximately $5B annualized transaction volume while positioning around earning globally, holding digitally, and spending locally. RedotPay reports more than 8m users and approximately $14B annualized payment volume. These are official company-reported competitive scale signals, not independent TAM or proof that all users maintain stablecoin balances. [S12–S13]

**Retention Hypothesis**

**Hypothesis:** A real active balance plus repeated routine spend could support a durable money relationship. The hypothesis is materially weakened by point-of-sale conversion, the near-term conversion/spend signal, and the lack of longitudinal balance evidence. KAST and RedotPay also show that the behavior already has strong alternatives. [S02, S04, S12–S13]

**Key Counter-evidence**

The Kansas City Fed estimates only 0.7% of stablecoin activity as payments; Visa explicitly warns that card usage proves spend but not long-term stablecoin balance; retail-sized transfers are not automatically retail payments; and the BVNK/YouGov sample is crypto-engaged rather than general population. [S01–S04]

**Evidence Strength**

**Medium** for a real user subset and spend behavior; **Low** for the prevalence of durable everyday balances, stablecoin-funded spend share, retention, and unique-user size.

**Unknowns**

Routine-frequency threshold; stablecoin versus volatile-crypto funding; recurring income versus savings source; card versus merchant/POS route; average balance; share of household spend; balance duration; country mix; switching willingness; local conversion and fee experience.

### C7. Crypto-gain-to-real-life spend converters

**Behavioral Definition**

An individual monetizes realized gains or proceeds from volatile crypto positions into real-life purchasing power, such as local fiat, a card purchase, or a merchant payment. The behavior is separated from a trader who keeps the proceeds in crypto/stablecoin and from C6’s routine spending out of a maintained stablecoin balance.

**Core Jobs**

Realize gains; convert crypto wealth into spendable value; pay an obligation or purchase; choose the timing and form of the conversion.

**Money Source**

**Fact / Inference:** Realized gains or proceeds from volatile crypto positions. This is not wage income, family support, or ordinary stablecoin payroll.

**Source Currency**

**Fact / Unknown:** The source asset is a volatile crypto asset or crypto-denominated proceeds; the exact token, acquisition currency, and fiat entry path are Unknown.

**Preferred Money Form**

**Inference:** Local fiat, a card-spend balance, or final merchant purchasing power at the moment of use. Stablecoin may be an intermediate liquidation or settlement form rather than the preferred retained form.

**Current Behavior**

**Market Evidence / User Evidence:** Visa describes crypto-linked card use and point-of-sale conversion of crypto/stablecoin into local fiat; 2025 card volume recovered and APAC issuer share led in the cited data. The BVNK/YouGov survey reports that 28% of holders convert or spend within days. These facts show a conversion-to-spend path, not a clean stablecoin-origin or routine-spend cohort. [S02, S04]

**Stablecoin Role**

Optional liquidation, settlement, or card-funding bridge between volatile crypto gains and final local purchasing power. It is not automatically a held balance.

**Frequency**

**Inference / Unknown:** Episodic or event-driven around realized gains, obligations, or opportunities to spend. Repeat behavior, cadence, and value are Unknown.

**Alternatives**

Exchange cash-out, bank/e-wallet withdrawal, P2P conversion, crypto cards, and ordinary card/payment rails. KAST and RedotPay are competitive examples of crypto-to-spend propositions, but their reported scale does not identify this exact cohort. [S12–S13]

**Market Evidence**

Visa provides the clearest rail evidence for crypto-linked spending and POS conversion. The Kansas City Fed’s 0.7% payment-function estimate provides a counterweight against treating all crypto liquidation or card volume as stablecoin payments. [S01–S03]

**Retention Hypothesis**

**Hypothesis:** Stablecoin retention after each event is likely limited when the user converts directly to local fiat or spends immediately. A repeat relationship may still exist if the user repeatedly realizes gains, but that is not the same as C6’s everyday balance retention.

**Key Counter-evidence**

Visa card volume is crypto-linked rather than a proof of stablecoin funding; POS conversion can eliminate a durable stablecoin balance; the near-term conversion signal does not prove gains, stablecoin, or everyday use; and retail-sized transfer data cannot be read as retail payment data. [S02–S04]

**Evidence Strength**

**Medium** for the existence of crypto-to-real-life spend rails; **Low** for stablecoin-specific share, user count, recurrence, and retained balance.

**Unknowns**

Stablecoin versus volatile-crypto funding share; gains versus principal; card versus cash/e-wallet route; recurrence; spend category; final currency; balance duration; user geography; willingness to keep funds in stablecoin after conversion.

### C8. Small digital businesses using stablecoin for cross-border settlement

**Behavioral Definition**

A sole proprietor or small digital business repeatedly receives client revenue, settles invoices, or pays contractors/suppliers across borders using stablecoin, while deciding whether to retain working capital or convert for operating expenses. The behavior is commercial cash-flow settlement, not personal salary receipt or family remittance.

**Core Jobs**

Receive client money; settle cross-border obligations; manage FX and payment friction; preserve or deploy working capital; pay contractors/suppliers.

**Money Source**

**Inference / Unknown:** Client invoices, business sales, or commercial receipts. The supplied evidence does not isolate sole proprietors or small digital businesses from larger B2B flows.

**Source Currency**

**Fact / Unknown:** USD, EUR, and GBP are explicit funding currencies in the Deel/BVNK payroll example, but no F-specific source-currency split is supplied. Local fiat and other invoice currencies remain Unknown. [S05]

**Preferred Money Form**

**Inference / Unknown:** Stablecoin may be preferred as a cross-border settlement or working-capital form, with local fiat/bank/e-wallet money used for operating expenses. No universal preference or retained-balance pattern is established.

**Current Behavior**

**Market Evidence:** Stablecoin transfers account for 29.3% of the Kansas City Fed activity estimate, and Chainalysis identifies cross-border utility in Sub-Saharan Africa. The contractor payroll opt-in evidence shows adjacent recurring digital settlement, but it does not prove small-business adoption. [S01, S05, S08]

**Stablecoin Role**

Invoice/settlement bridge and possible working-capital balance. It is distinct from personal everyday money even when the same owner later spends personally.

**Frequency**

**Inference / Unknown:** Repeated per invoice, client settlement, contractor payment, or supplier obligation; actual cadence, volume, and balance duration are Unknown.

**Alternatives**

Bank wires, payment platforms, Wise, Revolut, local bank accounts, and e-wallets. Wise’s scale and speed are evidence of strong cross-border alternatives, not evidence that small businesses use or prefer stablecoin. [S10–S11]

**Market Evidence**

Transfer and cross-border evidence supports the existence of a settlement job, but the supplied sources do not provide an independent count of sole proprietors or small digital businesses. The 10,000+ contractor payroll figure should not be relabeled as a small-business user count. [S01, S05, S08]

**Retention Hypothesis**

**Hypothesis / Unknown:** Working capital may remain in stablecoin between receipt and payment cycles, which could create a recurring balance relationship. Immediate conversion for payroll or expenses is equally plausible; no direct duration evidence is supplied.

**Key Counter-evidence**

The 29.3% transfer estimate mixes commercial, personal, trading, and other transfers; Sub-Saharan Africa evidence is market-level; contractor payroll is not small-business settlement; and generic cross-border alternatives are already scaled. [S01, S05, S08, S10–S11]

**Evidence Strength**

**Low–Medium** for the adjacent settlement behavior; **Low** for the specific small-business cohort, size, source-currency mix, retention, and stablecoin preference.

**Unknowns**

Personal versus business flow share; business size; sector; client/supplier geography; invoice currency; stablecoin share; conversion timing; working-capital duration; accounting/tax/compliance constraints; alternative-rail cost; willingness to switch.
