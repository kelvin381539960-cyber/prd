# Stage15 WS2 — Argentina H1 matched residual-gap research

> **Status:** `Argentina H1 outcome-coverage expression FALSIFIED_IN_CELL / normalized H1 NOT_YET_PASS`.
> **Evidence date:** public pages checked 2026-08-31; page publication/update dates are recorded per source.
> **Evidence class:** public product and help-centre evidence. Provider claims establish incumbent outcome coverage pressure, not user satisfaction, retention, or market size.
> **Stage boundary:** Included in final Stage15 Sol/high `CLEAN PASS`; normalized H1/H2 and Primary route selection remain unresolved.


## 1. Executive decision

**Decision: downrank Argentina as an H1 validation context; do not promote H1.**

The pre-registered incumbent falsifier is materially engaged in the Argentina cell for the broad “digital-dollar → local everyday purchasing power” expression:

- **belo** publicly documents Argentina-specific USD/crypto inflows, stablecoin crediting, local QR payments, a local prepaid card, local bank/CVU withdrawal, and service payments.
- **Lemon** publicly documents USD-to-USDT funding, Argentina QR payments with USDT/USDC/DAI and other assets, crypto-funded card payments, and local service payments.
- **KAST** has an Argentina-specific crypto-card page that names groceries, dining, online shopping, and Argentina acceptance; its global product pages connect stablecoin deposits, a dollar balance, and card spending.
- **Deel** has an Argentina-first stablecoin wallet for contractors, while belo publicly documents a Deel → USDC → local card/QR/service path. Deel alone is not current full H1 coverage because its stablecoin card is described as coming later in 2026; the combined alternative set nevertheless closes the source-to-use path for the same cohort.
- **RedotPay** adds generic stablecoin-to-card and local-currency-at-payment pressure. Argentina is not named in the current app, issuance, or physical-card delivery restriction lists, but a positive Argentina eligibility statement was not found, so this is lower-confidence corroboration rather than the decisive evidence.

This does **not** prove that every Argentina user is satisfied or that no narrower pain exists. No public source reviewed here links the same Argentina resident across repeated pay cycles, retained digital-dollar balances, local use, incumbent choice, and switching/parallel-use intent. The correct conclusion is therefore:

> **The broad Argentina H1 outcome-coverage gap is falsified in-cell by current incumbent coverage. A material relationship-level residual pain has not been established. The missing longitudinal user evidence remains an insufficiency, not a reason to invent a gap.**

`Primary strategic route` remains `NOT_YET_SELECTED`. This file does not kill H1 globally, select another geography, authorize a build, or change the roadmap.

## 2. Matched cohort and outcome

### 2.1 Target cohort used for the Argentina match

The same-cohort inclusion boundary for this slice is:

1. **Market:** resident in Argentina and eligible to use an Argentina-facing wallet/card or a product explicitly marketed for Argentina.
2. **Money source:** existing USDT/USDC or other digital-dollar value, or recurring cross-border/remote-work income that can arrive as USD/stablecoin. The test does not assume the wage currency.
3. **Use pattern required by the hypothesis:** needs repeated or meaningful local everyday purchasing power, not only trading, a one-off cash-out, travel spend, or transport-only remittance.
4. **Continuity question:** wants at least part of the value to remain digital-dollar/stablecoin-denominated until local use, or wants to avoid manually fully cashing out before each purchase.
5. **Everyday outcome:** groceries, restaurants/cafés, local retail, bills/services, transport, or comparable online/in-person everyday spend in Argentina.

This is a **research inclusion rule**, not a claim that cohort prevalence or repeat frequency is known. Exclude tourists, pure traders, users whose only job is immediate bank cash-out, and recipients for whom digital-dollar retention/reuse is not part of the job.

### 2.2 Matched H1 statement

`Digital-dollar / stablecoin value held or received → locally deep, everyday purchasing power in Argentina without requiring the user to fully cash out first.`

The comparison is made at the user-outcome level. Card, QR, bank transfer, local wallet, and off-ramp are mechanisms or alternatives; none is treated as a route or moat by itself.

### 2.3 Matched alternative stack

The relevant set is `belo, Lemon, KAST, RedotPay, Deel`, with `Wise/Stripe/Remote` retained as fiat-first or upstream/context alternatives. A product combination is allowed in the comparison when it is an actual public user path for the same cohort (for example, Deel payout into belo); it is not treated as a single-vendor feature.

### 4.1 First-class incumbent disconfirmation

The following are actual alternative outcome claims, not generic “stablecoin demand”:

1. belo and Lemon are Argentina-scoped products with stablecoin/dollar starting money and local QR/card/service outcomes.
2. KAST has an Argentina-scoped merchant-spend proposition and a stablecoin-linked global account/card proposition.
3. A user can combine Deel receipt with belo local use through a documented public path, so a missing single-vendor card in Deel does not itself create an unserved user outcome.
4. RedotPay demonstrates that even a generic international card can turn stablecoin value into local merchant purchasing power; its local eligibility limitation lowers confidence but does not create evidence of an AIX gap.

These findings falsify the broad claim that Argentina lacks a digital-dollar-to-local-spend solution. They do **not** falsify every narrower claim about price, trust, limits, failure recovery, local acceptance, tax treatment, or custody.

### 4.2 What was not found

No reviewed public source provides all of the following for the same Argentina target cohort:

- repeated income or stablecoin receipt across multiple cycles;
- balance held until later local use rather than immediate conversion/cash-out;
- local everyday spend frequency/value;
- current incumbent selected for the primary job;
- a documented failure or dissatisfaction with belo, Lemon, KAST, RedotPay, or the Deel→belo path; and
- a reason to switch or run AIX in parallel.

This is why this file does not claim `H0 globally wins` or `Argentina has zero residual pain`. It claims the stronger and decision-relevant negative finding: **no Argentina H1 residual pain has been evidenced after checking incumbents that already cover the outcome.**

## 6. Macro and activity signals explicitly excluded from the H1 decision

Argentina's inflation, crypto transaction activity, stablecoin purchase share, and provider co-location can justify why Argentina is worth red-teaming. They cannot establish this matched H1 gap:

- macro inflation does not show that the target cohort holds stablecoins until use;
- exchange or network volume does not show people, local everyday purchasing, or incumbent dissatisfaction;
- a contractor/stablecoin adoption ranking does not show that the same person later retains and spends locally; and
- generic stablecoin activity does not override direct evidence that Argentina-facing incumbents already provide the outcome.

Stage14's macro/network sources remain context-only for this WS2 slice. They are not counted as DR-1, DR-2, DR-4, or DR-5 passes.

## 7. Follow-up only if Argentina is retained as a red-team control

Public browsing should stop at this level unless a new, specific claim is defined. The only useful next test is same-cohort user research that recruits **existing belo/Lemon/KAST users and Deel→belo users**, then links one person across:

1. the same recurring receipt or held digital-dollar balance;
2. time held before use;
3. repeated local spend categories and value;
4. current incumbent and primary-vs-secondary role;
5. concrete failure/pain; and
6. willingness to switch or run AIX in parallel.

The research must pre-register the residual dimension before recruitment. “Wants stablecoins,” “inflation is high,” “needs a card,” and “more rails would be useful” are not sufficient residual-gap criteria.

## 8. Plain-language conclusion

Argentina is a strong **falsification market** for H1, not a clean white-space market. The exact outcome under test is already covered by local incumbents and by a source-to-local-use combination. Until a same-cohort user study demonstrates a material, repeated residual pain that these products do not solve, Argentina should not be used to promote H1 or justify an AIX feature/rail backlog.
