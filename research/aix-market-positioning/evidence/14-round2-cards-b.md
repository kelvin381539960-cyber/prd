# Strategic Validation 14 / Round 2 — Evidence Cards B

> **Research / access date:** 2026-08-31  
> **Scope:** Three Stage 14 user segments only: S4 recurring stablecoin remittance/family-money recipient, S5 existing stablecoin-balance everyday spender / crypto-card user, and S6 crypto-native user converting crypto gains/proceeds into real-life spend.  
> **Important label note:** S4–S6 in this file are **Stage 14 user-segment labels**. They are not the similarly named ecosystem strategy-cluster labels used in earlier market-map work.  
> **Decision boundary:** No Primary/Secondary selection, no capability prioritization, and no solution-level or AIX-specific product conclusion is made here.

## Reading rules

- The unit is a behavior-led user segment, not a wallet, transaction, provider customer count, nationality, or occupation label. Segments may overlap over time and must not be summed into one TAM.
- **Money Source** means why the person has the funds. **Source Currency** means the currency or asset before the stablecoin step. **Preferred Money Form** means what the person wants to keep or use. The latter must not be inferred from the former.
- **Unknown** means the reviewed evidence is insufficient; it does not mean absence.
- S4 is recipient-only. Sender evidence must not be projected onto the recipient.
- S5 requires evidence of an intentionally available stablecoin balance before repeated routine spending. A card transaction or point-of-sale conversion alone is insufficient.
- S6 requires realized crypto gains/proceeds and a real-life spending outcome. A trader/investor who only holds, trades, or rebalances remains a comparator, not S6.
- The six Round 2 supplemental sources below are the stopping point for external research. Inherited Stage 14 claims are cited by their existing claim IDs and source registers.

## User Segment S4 — Recurring stablecoin remittance / family-money recipient (recipient only)

**1. Segment Definition**  
**Fact / analyst definition:** An individual or household repeatedly receives personal or family support across a border through a transfer path in which stablecoin is used. This card covers the **recipient only**: it excludes the sender, self-transfer, commercial settlement, salary receipt, and a generic fiat-remittance recipient with no stablecoin behavior. A recipient who intentionally retains stablecoin may also overlap with the separate store-of-value behavior; that overlap must be evidenced, not assumed. [R1-C5; E14-2 §4.1, C18, C20]

**2. Core Jobs**  
Receive household support reliably; obtain usable local purchasing power; manage timing and FX/access friction; decide whether to cash out, use, or retain part of the received value. The sender's job and source of funds are not substitutes for the recipient's job.

**3. Money Source**  
**Fact / Inference / Unknown:** The incoming funds are sender-funded personal or family support. The recipient's own salary is not assumed, and the sender's underlying wage, savings, or other source is not identified at person level. [R1-C5]

**4. Source Currency**  
**Unknown:** The currency or asset before the stablecoin step may be local fiat, USD, or another form; the sender-side source currency and the recipient-side final currency are not established. Stablecoin may be only an intermediate transfer form. [R1-C5; E14-2:C18, C20]

**5. Preferred Money Form**  
**Inference / Unknown:** Local fiat, cash, bank money, or e-wallet value may be preferred for household use. A recipient may prefer to retain stablecoin for FX or value-protection reasons, but the reviewed evidence does not establish the split between immediate local payout and intentional stablecoin holding. [R1-C5; E14-2:C16–C20]

**6. Current Financial Behavior**  
**Inference / Unknown:** The defining financial behavior is recurring receipt of family/personal support, followed by some combination of local spending, cash-out, bank/e-wallet use, or retention. No recipient-level panel establishes the final form, amount, household-spend share, or cash-out timing. [R1-C5; E14-2:C18, C20, C22]

**7. Current Crypto Behavior**  
**Market Evidence / Unknown:** Stablecoin transfers are a material modeled function, and Chainalysis identifies cross-border and FX-related crypto use in Sub-Saharan Africa. These signals do not identify the recipient, prove that the recipient controls a wallet, or show that the recipient sees or retains stablecoin. [R2-01, R2-06; E14-2:C07, C18]

**8. Stablecoin Motivation**  
**Hypothesis / Market Evidence:** Potential motivations are faster or more accessible receipt, lower transfer friction, and easier cross-border value movement; if funds are retained, FX or nominal-value protection may matter. Chainalysis' directional cost-advantage evidence is a market estimate, not recipient preference or adoption. [E14-2:C20, C22; R2-06]

**9. Pain**  
**Market Evidence / Unknown:** The category has conventional-rail cost, delay, access, and FX pain. Recipient-side severity, failed-payment incidence, ability to choose a stablecoin route, and the value of any cost advantage are Unknown. A generic remittance-cost benchmark does not prove stablecoin demand. [E14-2:C20, C22]

**10. Frequency**  
**Inference / Unknown:** Recurring by definition, but actual cadence, seasonality, amount, number of senders, and corridor concentration are Unknown. “Recurring receipt” must not be inferred from one transfer or from aggregate transfer volume.

**11. Economic-Relationship Value**  
**Medium potential / Hypothesis:** Repeat receipt can create a recurring money relationship. Economic value is lower if the recipient immediately converts to local money and never returns to stablecoin; it is higher only if repeat receipt, repeat use, or intentional retained value is directly demonstrated. No AIX-specific value evidence is present.

**12. Alternatives**  
**Market Evidence / Inference:** Bank transfer, money-transfer operator, cash pickup, local bank, cash, and e-wallet rails are existing alternatives. Their relative use, recipient satisfaction, and switching behavior are not measured in the reviewed evidence. [E14-2:C22; R2-06]

**13. Why AIX**  
**Unknown / Hypothesis — no direct evidence:** The reviewed sources do not connect S4 recipients to AIX. AIX relevance would remain a hypothesis until recipient-side evidence shows that stablecoin-linked family receipt, local usability, or repeat value retention is an unresolved problem for a reachable cohort. This remains a validation question only.

**14. Why NOT AIX**  
**Unknown / Hypothesis:** If most recipients only need local fiat, cash, bank, or e-wallet value and never choose or retain stablecoin, existing remittance alternatives may satisfy the job without a stablecoin money relationship. This is a disconfirming hypothesis, not an AIX-specific fact.

**15. Market Size (category-relevant / reachable / unknown)**  
- **Category-relevant:** The Kansas City Fed models transfers as 29.3% of stablecoin activity, and Chainalysis reports substantial regional on-chain activity and cross-border/FX use. The inherited Round 1 evidence also records approximately $685B in remittance flows to LMICs and approximately $40B to the Philippines. These are market-level signals that mix remittance, self-transfer, trading, commercial, and other flows; they are not a count of stablecoin recipients. [R2-01, R2-06; R1-C4; E14-2:C07, C18, C20]
- **Reachable:** **Unknown.** No recipient-specific country/corridor denominator, stablecoin awareness measure, channel access measure, or AIX-specific reachable cohort is established.
- **Unknown:** Unique recipients; stablecoin share of remittance flows; sender relationship; source and destination currency; final payout form; immediate-conversion rate; retained balance; cadence and amount; overlap with store-of-value savers.

**16. Reachability**  
**Unknown:** Regional on-chain activity and provider availability cannot be converted into reachable recipients. The evidence does not establish recipient-side awareness, willingness to receive stablecoin, corridor-level access, or AIX-specific distribution reach.

**17. Retention**  
**Unknown / Hypothesis:** Repeat receipt could support a recurring relationship, but immediate local cash-out is equally plausible. No longitudinal recipient balance evidence shows that funds remain as stablecoin between receipt events. Retention must be separated from repeat receipt.

**18. Evidence Strength**  
**Medium** for the existence of a recipient-side cross-border money job and market-level stablecoin transfer utility; **Low** for stablecoin-specific recipient penetration, final-form preference, frequency, reachability, and retained balance.

**Disconfirming Evidence**  
The 29.3% transfer bucket includes non-remittance activity, and regional Chainalysis data includes non-recipient and potentially commercial flows. A stablecoin cost advantage does not prove that a recipient chooses stablecoin. If recipient research shows that stablecoin is invisible to the recipient, is converted immediately in nearly all cases, or is not available in the relevant corridor, S4 is better treated as a generic remittance-recipient comparator rather than a stablecoin user segment. [R2-01, R2-06; E14-2:C07, C18–C20]

**Validation Gate**  
Keep S4 as **Unknown / downstream validation required** until recipient-only evidence demonstrates: repeated family/personal receipt through a stablecoin path; the recipient's final money form; the share cashing out versus intentionally retaining; and a recurring recipient-side need independent of the sender's behavior. If the evidence shows only generic fiat receipt or sender-side stablecoin routing, do not upgrade this segment.

## User Segment S5 — Existing stablecoin-balance everyday spender / crypto-card user

**1. Segment Definition**  
**Fact / analyst definition:** An individual repeatedly uses an intentionally available stablecoin balance for routine personal purchases or obligations. A crypto card is only a rail or acquisition signal; the segment requires evidence that stablecoin was already held before spending and that routine spending repeats. A card payment that converts assets at point of sale without a retained stablecoin balance is not sufficient. [R1-C6; E14-2 §8, C03, C23]

**2. Core Jobs**  
Turn an active stablecoin balance into routine purchasing power; pay ordinary goods, services, or obligations; reduce friction between digital money and daily spending; keep a usable balance between spending events.

**3. Money Source**  
**Unknown:** Possible sources include stablecoin-paid income, savings, remittance receipt, an existing wallet balance, or realized crypto proceeds. The reviewed evidence does not provide a clean source-of-funds split for this segment. [R1-C6; E14-2:C02–C03]

**4. Source Currency**  
**Unknown:** The pre-stablecoin source may be local fiat, USD/EUR/GBP compensation, savings, or volatile crypto. Visa's point-of-sale conversion description explains a spending rail, not the consumer's original source currency. [R1-C6; R2-02]

**5. Preferred Money Form**  
**Inference:** The defining active form is a stablecoin balance held before the spending event. The merchant may receive local fiat after conversion, which does not change the user's pre-spend balance classification. If stablecoin is not retained before or between events, classify the behavior as an adjacent payment/conversion pattern, not S5. [R1-C6; R2-02, R2-04]

**6. Current Financial Behavior**  
**User Evidence / Unknown:** The BVNK/YouGov survey reports that 27% of surveyed stablecoin holders use stablecoins for everyday activities, but it does not define a frequency threshold, prove a pre-spend balance, or measure household-spend share. Current routine frequency and amount for the exact S5 cohort remain Unknown. [R2-03; E14-2:C01–C03]

**7. Current Crypto Behavior**  
**User Evidence / Market Evidence:** The segment holds stablecoin and spends repeatedly from that balance. Survey respondents report everyday use and interest in linked cards; Visa reports crypto-card activity recovering by the end of 2025. Both are broader than S5: Visa includes crypto generally, and the survey is self-reported without longitudinal balance linkage. [R2-02, R2-03; E14-2:C03, C14, C23]

**8. Stablecoin Motivation**  
**User Evidence / Hypothesis:** Reported motivations include lower fees, security, and global access. For S5 specifically, these may combine with convenience and the ability to use an existing dollar-like balance for ordinary purchases. The survey identifies reported motivations, not revealed preference or stablecoin retention. [R2-03; E14-2:C06]

**9. Pain**  
**User Evidence / Unknown:** The survey indicates demand for wider spending opportunities: more respondents want to spend crypto/stablecoins on major or lifestyle purchases than currently do. The evidence does not show whether the limiting pain is acceptance, cost, conversion, trust, balance availability, or a particular local-rail gap; there is no AIX-specific pain evidence. [R2-03; E14-2:C03]

**10. Frequency**  
**Inference / Unknown:** Routine and recurring by definition. The reviewed evidence does not establish the minimum event count, time window, spend share, average balance, or balance duration needed to label a person S5.

**11. Economic-Relationship Value**  
**Medium–High potential / Hypothesis:** An active balance plus repeated routine spend could support a durable money relationship. The hypothesis weakens materially when the rail converts at point of sale, when balances are small or short-lived, or when a user can move seamlessly to an existing card/account alternative. [R2-02, R2-04, R2-05; E14-2:C07, C23]

**12. Alternatives**  
**Market Evidence:** Ordinary bank and e-wallet balances, cash, conventional cards, exchange cash-out, and local payment rails substitute for the purchasing-power job. KAST describes a stablecoin-based account proposition with receive/send/spend from a unified balance and card use; RedotPay describes a stablecoin-based payment app, an all-in-one wallet, and conversion to local currency at payment. These are evidence of strong existing market alternatives, not segment-size evidence or recommendations. [R2-04, R2-05]

**13. Why AIX**  
**Unknown / Hypothesis — no direct evidence:** The reviewed sources contain no direct user evidence connecting S5 to AIX. AIX relevance would be a hypothesis only if target-market users can be shown to maintain stablecoin balances, spend routinely, and experience an unresolved strategic-level purchasing-power problem despite existing alternatives. No capability or solution is selected here.

**14. Why NOT AIX**  
**Unknown / Hypothesis:** Existing stablecoin account/card propositions may already satisfy the job, and point-of-sale conversion can satisfy the purchase without a durable stablecoin balance. If the observed behavior is mainly one-off card usage or just-in-time conversion, there is no evidence of an AIX-specific unmet relationship.

**15. Market Size (category-relevant / reachable / unknown)**  
- **Category-relevant:** The BVNK/YouGov sample reports 27% everyday stablecoin activity and 71% interest in a linked card; Visa reports crypto-card payment volume near prior highs by the end of 2025, with APAC leading by issuer-region share. These are sampled behavior and rail-activity signals, not S5 unique-user or durable-balance counts. [R2-02, R2-03; E14-2:C03, C14]
- **Reachable:** **Unknown.** The survey is crypto-engaged, provider propositions do not establish AIX reach, and no country-level count of users with a verified pre-spend stablecoin balance is available.
- **Unknown:** Durable-balance prevalence; routine-frequency threshold; average balance; stablecoin share of household spend; stablecoin versus volatile-crypto funding; card versus other rail; country mix; unique users; balance duration; switching willingness.

**16. Reachability**  
**Unknown:** Crypto engagement and provider-reported availability are not a target-market reach denominator. The evidence does not establish who can be reached, who already uses a competing account/card, or whether S5 behavior is present in the intended AIX cohort.

**17. Retention**  
**Unknown / Hypothesis:** Holding an intentional balance between repeated routine purchases could create retention, but no source links a stablecoin balance to repeated spending across time. Visa's point-of-sale conversion model and the survey's lack of longitudinal data leave direct balance retention unproven. [R2-02, R2-03]

**18. Evidence Strength**  
**Medium** for the existence of some stablecoin-related everyday-spend interest and card activity; **Low** for the strict S5 definition, durable balance prevalence, routine frequency, unique-user size, and retention.

**Disconfirming Evidence**  
The Kansas City Fed estimates only 0.7% of stablecoin function as payments while 48.8% is associated with trading assets; Philadelphia Fed analysis likewise places direct retail payments at a small share of its specified denominator. Visa explicitly allows digital assets to convert to local fiat at point of sale. Therefore card volume, payment intent, or a stablecoin purchase cannot be read as proof of an active retained balance. [R2-01, R2-02; E14-2:C07, C09, C23]

**Validation Gate**  
Keep S5 as **Unknown / downstream validation required** until target-market user or anonymous cohort evidence shows, across repeated periods: a stablecoin balance present before spending; routine personal spend from that balance; the source asset and final merchant-money form; and a balance relationship between events. If the evidence shows only point-of-sale conversion or a pre-funded non-stablecoin/card balance, exclude it from S5 and retain it only as an adjacent payment/conversion comparator.

## User Segment S6 — Crypto-native user converting crypto gains/proceeds into real-life spend

**1. Segment Definition**  
**Fact / analyst definition:** An individual realizes gains or proceeds from volatile crypto positions and converts them into real-life purchasing power for a personal purchase or obligation. The route may end in local fiat, a card purchase, or a merchant payment. S6 is separate from a trader/investor-only user who does not evidence real-life spend, and from S5's routine spending out of a maintained stablecoin balance. [R1-C7; E14-2 §8, C07, C23]

**2. Core Jobs**  
Realize crypto wealth; turn gains/proceeds into usable purchasing power; pay a real-life obligation or make a purchase; choose the timing and final money form of the conversion.

**3. Money Source**  
**Fact / Inference / Unknown:** The defining source is realized gains or proceeds from volatile crypto positions. The evidence does not cleanly distinguish gains from principal, nor identify whether the crypto was self-funded, received, or earned. It is not assumed to be salary, family support, or stablecoin payroll.

**4. Source Currency**  
**Fact / Unknown:** The source asset is volatile crypto or crypto-denominated proceeds; exact token, acquisition currency, and fiat entry path are Unknown. Stablecoin may appear as an intermediate liquidation or settlement form, but it is not automatically the source currency or retained form.

**5. Preferred Money Form**  
**Inference / Unknown:** The likely desired endpoint is local fiat, a card-spend balance, or final purchasing power at the moment of use. Stablecoin may be used as a bridge and then disappear from the user's balance; no universal preferred form is established.

**6. Current Financial Behavior**  
**Inference / Unknown:** The user makes an episodic real-life purchase or pays an obligation after realizing crypto proceeds. Repeat cadence, transaction amount, spend category, and share of personal spending are Unknown. The real-life spend event is required; liquidation without spending is not S6.

**7. Current Crypto Behavior**  
**Market Evidence / User Evidence / Unknown:** Visa documents crypto-linked card spending and instant point-of-sale conversion, while the BVNK/YouGov survey reports conversion/spending interest among a crypto-engaged sample. Neither source isolates realized gains, stablecoin funding, or a recurring S6 cohort. Trading and portfolio activity are adjacent but not substitutes for the S6 definition. [R2-02, R2-03; E14-2:C03, C07, C23]

**8. Stablecoin Motivation**  
**Unknown / Hypothesis:** If stablecoin is used, possible motivations are liquidity, settlement convenience, price stability during the short conversion window, or access to a spend rail. The reviewed evidence does not show that S6 users prefer stablecoin over direct crypto conversion, nor that they retain it after spending.

**9. Pain**  
**Inference / Unknown:** Potential pain includes timing and friction between crypto realization and usable purchasing power, plus conversion, FX, or access friction. Visa demonstrates a rail that can convert at point of sale, but not the user's realized pain, willingness to pay, or gains-specific behavior. [R2-02]

**10. Frequency**  
**Inference / Unknown:** Episodic or event-driven around realized gains, obligations, or opportunities to spend. The evidence does not establish repeat frequency, inter-event duration, or whether the same person returns after multiple gains events.

**11. Economic-Relationship Value**  
**Low–Medium potential / Hypothesis:** Repeated gains-to-spend events could create recurring transaction value, but immediate conversion and spending do not imply a durable stablecoin balance. Trader/investor-only activity may be more frequent yet remains outside S6 because it lacks the real-life spend job.

**12. Alternatives**  
**Market Evidence / Inference:** Exchange cash-out to bank or e-wallet, P2P conversion, ordinary cards, crypto-linked cards, and direct merchant/payment rails are alternatives. KAST and RedotPay show that crypto/stablecoin-to-spend propositions already exist; Visa demonstrates broad crypto-card activity. These are market alternatives only, not recommendations. [R2-02, R2-04, R2-05]

**13. Why AIX**  
**Unknown / Hypothesis — no direct evidence:** No reviewed source connects S6 users to AIX. AIX relevance would remain a hypothesis until direct evidence shows a repeatable, gains/proceeds-to-real-life-spend job that is not adequately served by exchange cash-out, existing cards, or ordinary payment rails. No solution or capability is selected here.

**14. Why NOT AIX**  
**Unknown / Hypothesis:** If users cash out directly, spend immediately through an existing card, or keep proceeds invested, the relationship may be episodic or trader/investor-only rather than a stablecoin money relationship. Existing alternatives may therefore satisfy the job; this is not an AIX-specific fact.

**15. Market Size (category-relevant / reachable / unknown)**  
- **Category-relevant:** The Kansas City Fed models 48.8% of stablecoin activity as associated with trading assets and 0.7% as payments; Visa reports substantial crypto-card activity and point-of-sale conversion; BVNK/YouGov reports everyday and broader crypto-spend behavior in a restricted crypto-engaged sample. These are adjacent signals, not a stablecoin-gains-to-spend user count. [R2-01–R2-03; E14-2:C03, C07, C23]
- **Reachable:** **Unknown.** No direct gains/proceeds cohort, country denominator, reachable channel, or AIX-specific reach evidence is available.
- **Unknown:** Realized-gain spenders; gains versus principal; stablecoin versus volatile-crypto funding; token and source mix; repeat rate; final currency/form; spend category; balance duration; geography; willingness to keep proceeds in stablecoin.

**16. Reachability**  
**Unknown:** Crypto-native survey eligibility and general card activity do not identify people who both realize gains and spend in real life. No evidence establishes a reachable target-market cohort or distinguishes this behavior from exchange cash-out and portfolio activity.

**17. Retention**  
**Unknown / Hypothesis:** Repeated realization events may produce repeat usage, but immediate local-fiat conversion or merchant settlement makes stablecoin retention unlikely to be assumed. No source provides a longitudinal link between gains, a stablecoin balance, and later routine spending.

**18. Evidence Strength**  
**Medium** for the existence of crypto-to-real-life spending rails; **Low** for the exact stablecoin-gains-specific segment, user size, recurrence, stablecoin preference, and retained balance.

**Disconfirming Evidence**  
Visa's card evidence covers crypto generally and explicitly allows point-of-sale conversion; the Kansas City Fed's largest function is trading-related, not payment-related; and the BVNK/YouGov survey does not identify realized gains as the source of funds. If a user only trades, holds, earns yield, or liquidates to fiat without a real-life spending event, the user is trader/investor-only or another comparator, not S6. [R2-01–R2-03; E14-2:C07, C23]

**Validation Gate**  
Keep S6 as **Unknown / downstream validation required** until direct user or anonymous cohort evidence links: a realized gain/proceeds event; a subsequent real-life purchase or obligation; gains versus principal; the use of stablecoin as an intermediate or retained form; and repeat behavior. If any observed cohort lacks the real-life spend outcome or is primarily trading/investment activity, do not classify it as S6.

## Cross-card boundary check

- **S4 recipient ≠ sender:** recipient-side source, final form, and retention remain separate unknowns.
- **S5 held balance → routine spend ≠ POS conversion:** a card rail can prove spend without proving stablecoin-as-money behavior.
- **S6 realized gains/proceeds → real-life spend ≠ trader/investor-only:** the real-life spending outcome is mandatory.
- **No Primary selected:** these cards provide evidence and validation gates only; they do not assign Primary, Secondary, or Non-target status.
